San Diego to Los Angeles on I-5

This guide covers I-5 from San Diego north to Los Angeles, exit 16 to exit 135, counting miles up from the Mexico border. You pass San Diego Bay, the coastal lagoons, about 18 miles of ocean beside Camp Pendleton, and the Orange County suburbs on the way. Gas, EV, food and lodging are listed as mapped in OpenStreetMap, not live.

Miles 16 to 20San Diego Bay and downtown

This stretch follows the east side of San Diego Bay through National City, the Navy's piers, Barrio Logan, downtown, the airport and Old Town.

  1. Exit 18 · Good stops

    El Indio

    El Indio began in 1940 as a family tortilla shop, and since 1947 it has served food from a spot on India Street that now sits beside the freeway.

  2. Exit 18 · on your left going north · History

    Lindbergh Field

    Jets landing at San Diego International Airport make a steep descent over the crest of Bankers Hill, close to the downtown towers, to reach a single runway.

  3. Exit 19 · on your right going north · History

    Old Town and Presidio Hill

    The hill east of the freeway is Presidio Hill, where Spain founded its first fort and mission in California in 1769, above Kosa'aay, a Kumeyaay village of 30 to 40 families.

Miles 20 to 31Mission Bay to La Jolla

Mission Bay's man-made islands, Rose Canyon and its fault, and the slopes of Mount Soledad line this stretch, along with UC San Diego and University City.

  1. Exit 21 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Mission Bay and San Diego River

    The San Diego River, crossed by the freeway near here, ends at the sea beside Mission Bay.

  2. Exit 26 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Mount Soledad and Rose Canyon

    The big hill west of the freeway is Mount Soledad, 823 feet high, with a 29-foot cross and TV towers on top.

  3. Exit 28 · Oddities

    San Diego California Temple

    The white building with two tall spires beside the freeway is the San Diego California Temple of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

Miles 31 to 44Torrey Pines to Encinitas

A string of coastal lagoons where creeks and rivers meet the sea, Los Peñasquitos, San Dieguito and San Elijo, alternates with the beach towns of Del Mar, Solana Beach and Encinitas.

  1. Exit 33 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Torrey Pines and Peñasquitos Lagoon

    West of the freeway, Los Peñasquitos Lagoon, Spanish for the little cliffs, spreads about 500 acres of marsh beside bluffs where Torrey pines grow, the rarest pine in North America.

  2. Exit 36 · on your left going north · History

    Del Mar racetrack

    The racetrack and fairgrounds beside the San Dieguito River are where the turf meets the surf.

  3. Exit 39 · Land & water

    San Elijo Lagoon

    The freeway crosses San Elijo Lagoon, a 915-acre reserve where Escondido Creek meets the sea.

  4. Exit 41 · Towns

    Encinitas, the little oaks

    Encinitas is Spanish for little oaks.

  5. Exit 43 · Orchards & farms

    Ecke poinsettia ranch

    The Ecke family moved their poinsettia business from Los Angeles to Encinitas in 1923, buying 40 acres at $150 an acre.

Miles 44 to 54Carlsbad and Oceanside

Carlsbad's flower and strawberry fields, three more lagoons, Batiquitos, Agua Hedionda and Buena Vista, and the beach town of Oceanside fill this stretch of Luiseño and Kumeyaay country.

  1. Exit 44 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Batiquitos Lagoon

    The freeway crosses Batiquitos Lagoon, 610 acres of tidal wetland where San Marcos Creek meets the sea.

  2. Exit 47 · on your right going north · Orchards & farms

    The Flower Fields

    In spring, the hillside east of the freeway turns bright with flowers.

  3. Exit 48 · Orchards & farms

    Carlsbad Strawberry Company

    The strawberry fields beside the freeway at Cannon Road belong to the Carlsbad Strawberry Company, a pick-your-own farm run by third-generation farmer Jimmy Ukegawa.

  4. Exit 49 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Agua Hedionda and the desal plant

    The freeway crosses Agua Hedionda Lagoon, Spanish for fetid water.

  5. Exit 50 · Towns

    Carlsbad's mineral water

    Carlsbad is named for a spa town in Bohemia.

  6. Exit 51 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Buena Vista Lagoon

    The freeway crosses Buena Vista Lagoon, the 223-acre border between Carlsbad and Oceanside and California's first ecological reserve.

Miles 54 to 70Camp Pendleton coast

Through Marine Corps Base Camp Pendleton the freeway follows the ocean for about 18 miles, past open hills, river valleys and the Surf Line railroad, on land kept for Marine training.

  1. Exit 54 · on your left going north · Towns

    Oceanside and its pier

    Oceanside's wooden pier, 1,942 feet long, is the second-longest wooden pier on the West Coast.

  2. Exit 55 · History

    Camp Pendleton

    The open hills along this stretch belong to Marine Corps Base Camp Pendleton, more than 125,000 acres with 17 miles of coastline, the last major undeveloped stretch of California coast south of Santa Barbara.

  3. Exit 57 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Santa Margarita River

    The freeway crosses the Santa Margarita River, one of the last free-flowing rivers in Southern California.

  4. Exit 59 · History

    The Surf Line railroad

    The railroad beside the freeway through Camp Pendleton is the Surf Line, named because in places it runs within 100 feet of the Pacific.

  5. Exit 62 · on your right going north · History

    Las Flores and Las Pulgas

    Near the Las Pulgas Road exit stands the Las Flores Adobe, a big U-shaped ranch house built in 1867 and 1868 by Marco Forster and now a National Historic Landmark.

  6. Exit 67 · History

    San Clemente Border Patrol checkpoint

    On the lanes toward San Clemente, about seven miles south of that city, stands a permanent Border Patrol checkpoint, one of 33 fixed checkpoints near the Mexican border.

  7. Exit 71 · on your left going north · Oddities

    San Onofre nuclear plant

    The San Onofre nuclear power plant, on the beach here, was long known for its two giant round domes, the containment buildings around its reactors.

Miles 70 to 76San Onofre and San Clemente

The freeway runs above San Onofre's beaches and the surf breaks of Trestles at the north edge of Camp Pendleton. The Orange County line is at Cristianitos Road, beside the hillside streets of Spanish-style San Clemente.

  1. Exit 71 · on your left going north · Good stops

    San Onofre and Trestles

    San Onofre, Spanish for Saint Onuphrius, a hermit from fourth-century Egypt, is a 3,000-acre state beach on Marine Corps land leased to California, with more than 2.5 million visitors a year.

  2. Exit 72 · on your left going north · History

    Nixon's Western White House

    On the bluff at Cotton's Point stands La Casa Pacifica, the house of peace, which President Richard Nixon bought in 1969.

  3. Exit 72 · on your right going north · History

    Panhe and Los Cristianitos

    Inland of the freeway, in the San Mateo campground area of San Onofre State Beach, is Panhe, the place at the water.

  4. Exit 76 · Towns

    Spanish Village by the Sea

    In 1925 Ole Hanson, a former mayor of Seattle, laid out a 2,000-acre town on old rancho land by the ocean and named it after San Clemente Island.

Miles 76 to 86Dana Point and Capistrano Valley

Between Dana Point and Mission Viejo the freeway runs inland through the valley of San Juan and Trabuco creeks, Acjachemen homeland, past the mission town of San Juan Capistrano, with the ranch lands of Rancho Mission Viejo to the east.

  1. Exit 79 · on your left going north · History

    Dana Point and the hides

    West of the freeway on Pacific Coast Highway, Dana Point is named for Richard Henry Dana Jr., who sailed here in 1835 on the brig Pilgrim and wrote about it in Two Years Before the Mast.

  2. Exit 83 · on your left going north · History

    Putuidem village

    Just off the freeway, about a mile north of the mission, stood Putuidem, a mother village of the Acjachemen people that gave rise to other villages.

  3. Exit 83 · Land & water

    Trabuco Creek's lost gun

    The freeway crosses Arroyo Trabuco here, a 22-mile creek that runs out of the Santa Ana Mountains to meet San Juan Creek beside the mission.

Miles 86 to 95Saddleback Valley

Suburbs of Mission Viejo, Laguna Hills and Lake Forest fill the Saddleback Valley below the mountain's two peaks, and Interstate 405 meets this freeway at the El Toro Y.

  1. Exit 86 · Orchards & farms

    Rancho Mission Viejo

    East of San Juan Capistrano, between Ladera Ranch and San Clemente, lies Rancho Mission Viejo, the old mission ranch, still about 23,000 acres.

  2. Exit 89 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Saddleback

    On clear days, the two-humped mountain east of the freeway is Saddleback.

  3. Exit 94 · Oddities

    El Toro Y

    This is the El Toro Y, where Interstate 5 and Interstate 405 meet.

  4. Exit 95 · on your right going north · History

    El Toro Marine air base

    East of the freeway, the Marines flew from El Toro air station from 1942 until it closed in 1999.

Miles 95 to 103Irvine Ranch and Tustin

The freeway crosses the flat land of the old Irvine Ranch and passes Tustin's old Marine base.

  1. Exit 97 · Orchards & farms

    The Irvine Ranch

    The land around here was part of the Irvine Ranch, which once stretched 23 miles from the ocean to the Santa Ana River.

  2. Exit 100 · Oddities

    Jamboree Road

    Jamboree Road is named for a Boy Scout campout.

  3. Exit 101 · on your left going north · Oddities

    Tustin's giant blimp hangar

    South of the freeway stands one of two giant wooden hangars the Navy built during World War II for blimps that patrolled the coast.

Miles 103 to 112Santa Ana and Anaheim

The freeway crosses the Santa Ana River and the Orange Crush interchange and passes Christ Cathedral and Disneyland, on a plain that was vineyards in the 1800s and orange groves into the 1950s.

  1. Exit 103 · Orchards & farms

    Orange County's orange groves

    In 1936 Orange County grew a sixth of the nation's Valencia oranges, and by 1948 it had 67,263 acres of them, more than five million trees.

  2. Exit 104 · Good stops

    Santa Ana Zoo's 50 monkeys

    Right beside the freeway, in Prentice Park, is the Santa Ana Zoo, which opened on March 8, 1952.

  3. Exit 105 · on your left going north · Towns

    Santa Ana, the county seat

    West of the freeway, Santa Ana grew on Rancho Santiago de Santa Ana, granted in 1810 to José Antonio Yorba, a sergeant in the Spanish army.

  4. Exit 107 · Land & water

    Santa Ana River

    The wide channel under the freeway here is the Santa Ana River, the largest river entirely within Southern California, running 96 miles from the San Bernardino Mountains to the sea.

  5. Exit 107 · on your left going north · Oddities

    The Crystal Cathedral

    The huge glass building west of the freeway is Christ Cathedral, built as the Crystal Cathedral and finished in 1980.

  6. Exit 109 · on your right going north · Good stops

    Angel Stadium and the Big A

    East of the freeway, near Katella Avenue, is Angel Stadium, home of the Los Angeles Angels since 1966.

  7. Exit 110 · on your left going north · Good stops

    Disneyland

    Just southwest of the freeway is Disneyland, built on 160 acres of orange groves and walnut trees, a site picked by researchers Disney hired to forecast where the region's population would grow.

  8. Exit 111 · on your right going north · Towns

    Anaheim's German vineyards

    Anaheim was founded in 1857 by 50 German families from San Francisco, who bought some 1,160 acres near the Santa Ana River for 2 dollars an acre to grow grapes.

Miles 112 to 124Buena Park to Norwalk

Between Buena Park and Downey the freeway crosses the line between Orange and Los Angeles counties and runs through the Gateway Cities of La Mirada, Santa Fe Springs and Norwalk, on Rancho Los Nietos land that later held olive groves, dairies and oil fields.

  1. Exit 116 · on your left going north · Good stops

    Knott's chicken and boysenberries

    About a mile and a half south on Beach Boulevard, Knott's Berry Farm began in 1923 as a roadside berry stand.

  2. Exit 118 · on your right going north · Towns

    La Mirada's olive ranch

    La Mirada, Spanish for the look, began as the ranch of Andrew McNally, the Chicago printer and mapmaker of Rand McNally.

  3. Exit 121 · on your left going north · History

    Norwalk and Rancho Los Nietos

    In 1784 the Spanish governor gave a former soldier, Manuel Nieto, the use of all the land between the Santa Ana and Los Angeles rivers, from Mission San Gabriel down to the sea.

  4. Exit 123 · on your right going north · History

    Santa Fe Springs gusher

    Santa Fe Springs takes its name from mineral springs that Dr. James Fulton turned into a sulfur spa in the 1870s, and that the Santa Fe Railway bought in 1886.

Miles 124 to 134Downey, Commerce and East LA

The freeway crosses the San Gabriel River and the Rio Hondo and runs through aerospace Downey, the rail-side factories of Commerce, and East Los Angeles.

  1. Exit 124 · Land & water

    San Gabriel River and Rio Hondo

    The freeway crosses the San Gabriel River here, beside the 605 junction, and the Rio Hondo, Spanish for deep river, about two and a half miles to the northwest.

  2. Exit 125 · on your left going north · History

    Downey, Apollo and McDonald's

    Downey, southwest of the freeway, is named for John Gately Downey, the youngest governor California ever had.

  3. Exit 129 · on your right going north · Oddities

    The Citadel

    That long wall decorated like an ancient palace was built in 1929 as the Samson Tire and Rubber Company factory.

  4. Exit 133 · Towns

    Downtown Los Angeles skyline

    The towers of downtown Los Angeles rise to the west, across the Los Angeles River.

  5. Exit 134 · on your left going north · History

    The Sears tower

    The tall Art Deco tower near the interchange belongs to the old Sears, Roebuck mail-order building, finished in 1927 in just 171 days.

Miles 134 to 135Boyle Heights to Elysian Park

The freeway threads the East Los Angeles Interchange in Boyle Heights, runs through Lincoln Heights, the city's first suburb, and crosses the Los Angeles River where the Arroyo Seco joins it, beside the wooded hills of Elysian Park.

  1. Exit 134 · on your left going north · Oddities

    East Los Angeles Interchange

    This tangle of ramps is the East Los Angeles Interchange, where Interstate 5, Interstate 10, U.S. 101 and State Route 60 meet on 135 acres of Boyle Heights.

Questions drivers ask

Where does I-5 run along the ocean through Camp Pendleton?

Between roughly mile 54 and mile 70, the freeway follows the ocean for about 18 miles through Marine Corps Base Camp Pendleton. The Surf Line railroad runs beside it, and the Aliso Creek Rest Area at mile 59.4 serves both directions.

What exit is Disneyland on I-5?

Disneyland is just southwest of the freeway near exit 110. The data lists Harbor Boulevard at exit 110A, with 21 lodging places and 26 food places mapped there.

Where can I find gas on I-5 in Orange County?

Gas stations are mapped at many exits, for example Crown Valley Parkway (exit 86), La Paz Road (exit 89), El Toro Road (exit 91) and Red Hill Avenue (exit 101B). This is mapped data, not live, so check a stop's hours and prices yourself.

Where are EV chargers along I-5 from San Diego to Los Angeles?

EV chargers are mapped at 66 exits in this stretch, including Palomar Airport Road (exit 47), Vista Way and Cassidy Street (exit 51C) and Lake Forest Drive (exit 92A). Networks listed include Tesla Supercharger, Electrify America, EVgo and ChargePoint, as mapped, not live.
