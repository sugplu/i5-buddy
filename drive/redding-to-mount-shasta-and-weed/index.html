Redding to Mount Shasta and Weed on I-5

This stretch of I-5 runs from Redding at exit 677 up past Shasta Lake, through the forested Sacramento River canyon, beneath the granite spires of Castle Crags and on to Mount Shasta and Weed. I-5 Buddy talks you through what you pass along the way, from the Carr Fire to Hedge Creek Falls and Pluto's Cave. Gas, EV, food and lodging shown here come from OpenStreetMap as mapped, not live.

Miles 677 to 680Redding

Redding sits at the northwestern end of the Central Valley, with mountains on the north, east and west and farmland to the south.

  1. Exit 677 · History

    The Carr Fire

    On July 26, 2018, the Carr Fire jumped the Sacramento River into the west side of Redding, and 38,000 people had to evacuate. Full story

  2. Exit 678 · on your left going north · Towns

    Redding, once Poverty Flats

    During the Gold Rush, miners found little gold in this ground, and the spot was called Poverty Flats. Full story

  3. Exit 678 · on your left going north · Oddities

    Sundial Bridge

    West of the freeway, the Sundial Bridge, a 700-foot footbridge designed by Santiago Calatrava, crosses the Sacramento River. Full story

Miles 680 to 688North Redding and the dam towns

Here Redding's northern edge meets the city of Shasta Lake, with Shasta Dam a few miles west.

  1. Exit 680 · on your left going north · History

    Old Shasta

    Highway 299 leads to Shasta, about six miles west of Redding. Full story

  2. Exit 682 · Oddities

    The 9.5-mile conveyor belt

    To make concrete for Shasta Dam, builders needed mountains of sand and gravel. Full story

  3. Exit 684 · on your left going north · Towns

    Shasta Lake, the dam towns

    The city of Shasta Lake grew out of five small communities that sprang up in 1938, when work began on Shasta Dam: Central Valley, Project City, Summit City, Pine Grove and Toyon. Full story

  4. Exit 685 · on your left going north · History

    Shasta Dam

    Shasta Dam stands about four miles west, 602 feet tall. Full story

  5. Exit 687 · on your left going north · History

    Kennett, the drowned town

    Under Shasta Lake lies Kennett, once the largest mining town in the area outside Redding and Shasta. Full story

Miles 688 to 704Shasta Lake

The freeway crosses Shasta Lake, whose steep shores are covered with tall evergreens and manzanita.

  1. Exit 688 · Oddities

    The drowned highway

    Before the lake, old Highway 99 ran through Mountain Gate and then plunged into the valley that Shasta Lake now fills. Full story

  2. Exit 690 · History

    Bass Hill stage road

    Near the Bridge Bay exit stands a marker to the stagecoach days. Full story

  3. Exit 689 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Shasta Lake

    Shasta Lake is California's largest reservoir, with 365 miles of steep, wooded shoreline. Full story

  4. Exit 691 · History

    Pit River Bridge

    The Pit River Bridge is a double-decker. Full story

  5. Exit 693 · Oddities

    The giant median

    Just north of the Pit River Bridge, the two directions of I-5 split apart, leaving what is said to be the second-largest median in California. Full story

  6. Exit 694 · Land & water

    Shasta salamanders

    The limestone hills around this lake hide salamanders found nowhere else on Earth. Full story

  7. Exit 695 · on your right going north · Good stops

    Lake Shasta Caverns

    Getting to these limestone caves takes a catamaran ride across the lake's McCloud arm and then a bus ride up a steep mountain road. Full story

  8. Exit 696 · History

    Winnemem Wintu homeland

    The McCloud River arm of this lake is the homeland of the Winnemem Wintu, whose name means middle water people or middle river people. Full story

  9. Exit 698 · on your right going north · History

    The Salt Fire

    On June 30, 2021, a fire started beside I-5 near the Salt Creek exit and shut down the freeway. Full story

  10. Exit 700 · History

    The first federal fish hatchery

    In 1872, the nation's first federal fish hatchery opened on the McCloud River, which now flows into this lake. Full story

  11. Exit 702 · on your left going north · Towns

    Lakehead, top of the lake

    Lakehead sits on the northern shore of Shasta Lake. Full story

Miles 704 to 722Sacramento River canyon

The freeway follows a steep, forested canyon, past old stage stops and Gold Rush camps.

  1. Exit 707 · History

    Delta, end of the line

    When the railroad pushed up this canyon in the 1880s, the town of Delta was the end of the line for about two years. Full story

  2. Exit 711 · Land & water

    The upper Sacramento River

    The river beside the freeway is the upper Sacramento. Full story

  3. Exit 712 · History

    Portuguese Flat

    Near the Pollard Flat exit was Portuguese Flat, a Gold Rush camp of the early 1850s made up largely of Portuguese miners. Full story

  4. Exit 715 · History

    River, rails and road

    The river, the railroad and the freeway all squeeze through this one canyon. Full story

  5. Exit 718 · History

    Southern's stage station

    The name of this exit goes back to Simeon Southern, nicknamed Sims, who opened a log stage station and hotel here in 1859, where travelers stopped to rest and eat for 50 years. Full story

  6. Exit 720 · History

    The Siskiyou Trail

    Long before this was a freeway, it was a web of Native American footpaths between California and the Northwest. Full story

Miles 722 to 735Castle Crags and Dunsmuir

Granite spires rise west of the freeway, and an old railroad town fills the canyon bottom. Dunsmuir gets about 63 inches of rain and snow a year, about three and a half times as much as Yreka or Medford.

  1. Exit 723 · on your left going north · History

    Homeland and the 1855 battle

    The country around Castle Crags is the ancestral home of the Okwanuchu Shasta people. Full story

  2. Exit 724 · Good stops

    Castle Crags State Park

    In the 1890s, mineral water from a spring here was bottled and shipped all over the world. Full story

  3. Exit 726 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Castle Crags

    These granite spires rise from about 2,000 feet at the river to more than 6,500 feet at the top. Full story

  4. Exit 728 · on your left going north · Good stops

    Sleep in a caboose

    Just off this exit, a lodge founded in 1968 lets guests sleep in old cabooses and eat in a restaurant inside an original train car, with Castle Crags towering above. Full story

  5. Exit 729 · Towns

    Dunsmuir, the railroad town

    Dunsmuir was a railroad town first, nicknamed Pusher, because extra steam engines were added here to push trains up the steep grades north of town. Full story

  6. Exit 730 · History

    Upper Soda Springs

    Under the freeway in north Dunsmuir lies Upper Soda Springs, now a riverside park. Full story

  7. Exit 731 · Oddities

    Babe Ruth in Dunsmuir

    In 1924, New York Yankees stars Babe Ruth and Bob Meusel came to Dunsmuir on a barnstorming tour and played an exhibition game against the locals in the city park. Full story

  8. Exit 732 · Good stops

    Hedge Creek Falls

    A short trail from this exit leads to a waterfall with a small cave behind it, so you can walk behind the falling water. Full story

  9. Exit 733 · on your left going north · Oddities

    Where Shasta soda began

    Just north of Dunsmuir was Shasta Springs, a summer resort on the railroad main line in the late 1800s and early 1900s. Full story

  10. Exit 734 · on your left going north · History

    The Cantara Loop spill

    On the night of July 14, 1991, a train derailed at a horseshoe curve called the Cantara Loop, upstream from Dunsmuir, and a tank car spilled about 19,000 gallons of pesticide into the river. Full story

Miles 735 to 746Mount Shasta and Black Butte

The canyon opens onto the foot of the volcano, with the city of Mount Shasta, the dark cone of Black Butte and Black Butte Summit.

  1. Exit 736 · on your right going north · Towns

    McCloud, a company town

    Highway 89 heads east to McCloud, a lumber company town on the southern slope of Mount Shasta. Full story

  2. Exit 737 · on your left going north · Orchards & farms

    Mount Shasta trout hatchery

    On Old Stage Road in Mount Shasta, the Mount Shasta Hatchery has raised trout since 1888, and the state calls it the oldest trout hatchery west of the Mississippi. Full story

  3. Exit 738 · on your right going north · Towns

    Mount Shasta City

    This town was called Strawberry Valley, then Berryvale, then Sisson, after Justin Sisson, who gave the land for the railroad station. Full story

  4. Exit 739 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Headwaters of the Sacramento

    In Mount Shasta City Park, water pours straight out of a hillside at Big Springs. Full story

  5. Exit 740 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Mount Shasta

    Mount Shasta rises to 14,179 feet. Full story

  6. Exit 741 · Oddities

    The Lemurians legend

    Mount Shasta has a famous legend: a hidden city on the mountain, home to advanced beings from a lost continent called Lemuria. Full story

  7. Exit 742 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Black Butte

    The dark cone close beside the freeway is Black Butte, a cluster of lava domes that squeezed out about 9,000 to 10,000 years ago. Full story

  8. Exit 743 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Shastina and Diller Canyon

    The smaller peak on Mount Shasta's west side is Shastina, about 12,330 feet tall. Full story

  9. Exit 744 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Mount Eddy

    The big peak west of the freeway is Mount Eddy, 9,037 feet tall, the highest point in the Trinity Mountains. Full story

Miles 746 to 756Weed, lumber and wind

Weed grew up around lumber mills. Hot flows from Shastina's eruptions once covered the ground here, and recent wildfires have left their marks.

  1. Exit 747 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Pluto's Cave

    About 12 miles northeast of Weed is Pluto's Cave, a lava tube roughly 190,000 years old. Full story

  2. Exit 747 · on your right going north · Towns

    Weed, a windy lumber town

    Weed is named for Abner Weed, who found that the strong winds here were good for drying lumber. Full story

  3. Exit 749 · on your right going north · History

    Weed's wildfires

    On September 15, 2014, the wind-driven Boles Fire spread through Weed and destroyed more than 150 buildings, most of them homes, in a single day. Full story

  4. Exit 748 · Oddities

    A two-block highway

    This exit is the end of State Route 265, North Weed Boulevard. Full story

  5. Exit 751 · Land & water

    Gray wolves return

    Gray wolves have returned to Siskiyou County. Full story

  6. Exit 755 · History

    Old Highway 99

    A two-lane county road called Old Highway 99 runs between Weed and Yreka, still in the same form it had when it was U.S. 99, the main West Coast highway before I-5. Full story

Miles 756 to 757Shasta Valley

A wide, dry valley in the rain shadow of the Klamath Mountains, dotted with hummocks, with hay fields, cattle and juniper.

Questions drivers ask

What exit is Hedge Creek Falls on I-5?

Hedge Creek Falls is at exit 732, near Dunsmuir. A short trail from the exit leads to a waterfall with a small cave behind it, so you can walk behind the falling water.

Where can I find gas between Redding and Weed on I-5?

In the data, 19 exits in this stretch have gas listed, from Cypress Avenue at exit 677 up to North Weed Boulevard at exit 748. Gas is mapped in OpenStreetMap, not live, so it does not tell you what is open.

Which exits on I-5 near Mount Shasta and Weed have EV charging mapped?

Central Mount Shasta at exit 738 lists ChargePoint, Tesla Supercharger and Rivian networks, South Weed at exit 745 lists ChargePoint, EVgo and Tesla Supercharger, and North Weed Boulevard at exit 748 lists Electric Era and Tesla Supercharger. These are as mapped, not live.

Are there rest areas on I-5 between Redding and Weed?

The data lists three: O'Brien Rest Area at mile 692.9, Lakehead Rest Area at mile 704.7, and Weed Airport Rest Area at mile 753.1, which is listed for both directions. The direction for the first two is not stated in the data.
