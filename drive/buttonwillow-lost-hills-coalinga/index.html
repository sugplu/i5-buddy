Buttonwillow, Lost Hills and Coalinga stops on I-5

This stretch of I-5 runs from about mile 255 to 340 across open valley farmland, oil country and the West Side of Fresno County. The guide lists the exits with mapped gas, EV charging, food and lodging, along with the rest areas and truck stops, all as mapped in OpenStreetMap and not live. Miles are California exit numbers counting up from the Mexico border.

Miles 255 to 262Kern River and the Elk Hills

The freeway crosses the Kern River near the Kern Water Bank's recharge basins and runs past the low, bare Elk Hills.

  1. Exit 257 · History

    Henry Miller, the Cattle King

    Buttonwillow was a headquarters of Miller and Lux, the company of Henry Miller, known as the Cattle King of California. Full story

  2. Exit 262 · on your left going north · Oddities

    Midway Substation

    The big electrical yard about a mile east of Buttonwillow is Midway Substation. Full story

  3. Exit 262 · on your left going north · Towns

    The Buttonwillow tree

    The town of Buttonwillow is named for a single tree, and it is not a willow at all. Full story

Miles 262 to 278Buttonwillow cotton country

Around Buttonwillow, known locally as cotton country, farm fields and big power lines spread across the valley floor, with the Temblor Range on the far western skyline.

  1. Exit 262 · on your right going north · Oddities

    California's balance point

    About 7 miles northeast of here lies the center of population of California. Full story

  2. Exit 263 · on your left going north · Land & water

    McKittrick Tar Pits

    West of Buttonwillow, near the tiny town of McKittrick, natural asphalt oozes out of the ground from the oil field below. Full story

  3. Exit 268 · Orchards & farms

    Cotton country

    Cotton has deep roots on this side of the valley. Full story

  4. Exit 268 · on your left going north · Land & water

    The Temblor Range

    On clear days, the mountains along the far western skyline are the Temblor Range, named from the Spanish word for tremor. Full story

  5. Exit 273 · Land & water

    San Joaquin kit fox

    This dry west side of the valley is home to the San Joaquin kit fox, an endangered fox with huge ears. Full story

  6. Exit 275 · on your left going north · Oddities

    Downhill all the way

    A few miles west, the California Aqueduct flows downhill by gravity, with no pumps. Full story

Miles 278 to 292Lost Hills oil and pistachios

Oil pumps dot the low Lost Hills west of the freeway, the aqueduct runs past the town of Lost Hills, and Kern National Wildlife Refuge lies out on the flats to the east.

  1. Exit 278 · on your left going north · History

    Blackwells Corner

    West on Highway 46, where it meets Highway 33, Blackwells Corner is named for George Blackwell, who started a rest stop there in 1921. Full story

  2. Exit 278 · on your left going north · Towns

    Lost Hills

    Lost Hills, a town of about 2,400 people a couple of miles west of the freeway, takes its name from the low Lost Hills range beside it. Full story

  3. Exit 280 · on your left going north · History

    Lost Hills oil field

    In July 1910, two drillers named Martin and Dudley were drilling a water well for livestock in the low hills west of here. Full story

  4. Exit 284 · Land & water

    Tule fog season

    From November through March, tule fog can fill this southern end of the valley. Full story

  5. Exit 288 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Kern National Wildlife Refuge

    East of here, Kern National Wildlife Refuge protects 11,249 acres of desert uplands, an old river corridor and marsh on the southern edge of what was once Tulare Lake. Full story

Miles 292 to 309Into Kings County

North of the Kern County line, the old bed of Tulare Lake, now farmland, spreads east of the freeway.

  1. Exit 293 · History

    Kern and Kings county line

    The line between Kern and Kings counties crosses the freeway here. Full story

  2. Exit 296 · Land & water

    Valley fever in the dust

    The soils of the San Joaquin Valley, especially its southern end, can hold Coccidioides, a fungus that causes valley fever, also called San Joaquin Valley fever. Full story

  3. Exit 300 · on your left going north · Oddities

    The Coastal Branch

    A few miles west of here, a branch of the California Aqueduct splits off and heads for the coast. Full story

  4. Exit 305 · on your right going north · Towns

    Alpaugh and Atwell's Island

    Utica Avenue leads east to Alpaugh, a town on what was once Atwell's Island, the largest island in Tulare Lake. Full story

  5. Exit 305 · on your right going north · History

    Colonel Allensworth's town

    About 30 miles east, past Alpaugh, Colonel Allensworth State Historic Park preserves the first California town founded, financed and governed by African Americans. Full story

  6. Exit 309 · Oddities

    Crossing the California Aqueduct

    The freeway crosses the California Aqueduct here, the concrete river that carries water from the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta toward Southern California. Full story

Miles 309 to 319Kettleman Hills

The Kettleman Hills, with their oil field, rise west of the freeway.

  1. Exit 309 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Ghost of Tulare Lake

    East of here lies the bed of Tulare Lake, once the largest freshwater lake west of the Mississippi River. Full story

  2. Exit 309 · on your left going north · History

    A landfill and a town's fight

    Off Highway 41, about 3.5 miles southwest of Kettleman City, the Kettleman Hills facility is a landfill of about 1,500 acres for hazardous and household waste, run by a subsidiary of Waste Management. Full story

  3. Exit 309 · on your right going north · Good stops

    Kettleman City, halfway

    Kettleman City is near the halfway point between Los Angeles and San Francisco or Sacramento, which makes this exit a major stop for food and lodging. Full story

  4. Exit 314 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Kettleman Hills

    The long, low hills along the west side are the Kettleman Hills, about 30 miles long and up to about 1,200 feet high. Full story

  5. Exit 319 · on your left going north · History

    The 1928 Kettleman gusher

    In October 1928, after 19 months of drilling, the Milham Exploration Company's Elliott Number 1 well struck oil more than 7,000 feet under the Kettleman Hills. Full story

Miles 319 to 330Fresno County's West Side

The freeway runs through the West Side farmland of Fresno County, which in 2022 led California counties in the value of its almonds, pistachios, garlic and processing tomatoes.

  1. Exit 319 · on your left going north · Towns

    Avenal, the oat field

    Highway 269 crosses the Kettleman Hills to Avenal, whose name is Spanish for oat field. Full story

  2. Exit 322 · Orchards & farms

    Garlic country

    Fresno County grows most of the garlic in the United States, and west side towns such as Huron picked up the slack as Gilroy, once the country's biggest garlic producer, ran out of room. Full story

  3. Exit 325 · on your right going north · Oddities

    Gates Substation bottleneck

    The sprawling electrical yard east of the freeway is Gates Substation, a key junction on the power lines linking Northern and Southern California. Full story

  4. Exit 325 · on your right going north · Towns

    Huron, harvest town

    East of the freeway is Huron, a farm town of about 6,200 people that swells to more than 15,000 at harvest time, when migrant farmworkers arrive. Full story

  5. Exit 327 · History

    Arroyo Pasajero bridges

    The freeway bridges here cross Los Gatos Creek, once called Arroyo Pasajero, the travelers' creek. Full story

Miles 330 to 340Coalinga plains

Wide-open farmland stretches east of the freeway, with the oil field ridges around Coalinga and the Diablo Range to the west, and the big Harris Ranch feedlot beside the freeway.

  1. Exit 334 · on your right going north · Good stops

    Harris Ranch Inn and restaurant

    The Harris Ranch restaurant opened beside the freeway in 1977, aimed at local farmers, and soon became a popular stop for travelers on the busy freeway between Los Angeles and San Francisco. Full story

  2. Exit 334 · on your left going north · History

    Coalinga Oil Field

    The long, low ridge between the freeway and Coalinga is Anticline Ridge, part of the Coalinga Oil Field, one of only six California fields to have produced more than a billion barrels of oil. Full story

  3. Exit 337 · on your left going north · Towns

    Coalinga and the Iron Zoo

    Coalinga began in 1888 as a Southern Pacific coaling stop for steam locomotives. Full story

  4. Exit 337 · on your left going north · History

    The 1983 Coalinga earthquake

    At 4:42 on the afternoon of May 2, 1983, a magnitude 6.2 earthquake struck Coalinga. Full story

  5. Exit 337 · on your right going north · Orchards & farms

    Harris Ranch feedlot

    Harris Ranch is California's largest beef producer, and its feedlot sits right beside the freeway. Full story

Questions drivers ask

Where are the rest areas on I-5 between Buttonwillow and Coalinga?

Two are mapped here: Buttonwillow Rest Area at mile 259.2 and Coalinga-Avenal Rest Area at mile 320.2, both listed for both directions.

Which I-5 exits have gas between Buttonwillow and Coalinga?

Seven exits in this range have gas mapped: 257, 278, 305, 309, 319, 325 and 334. Exits 278 and 309 list the most stations, 14 and 9 respectively, with exit 257 also listing 9.

Where can I charge an EV on I-5 near Lost Hills and Kettleman City?

Charging is mapped at four exits in this range: 257, 278, 309 and 334. Exit 278 for Lost Hills lists Electrify America and Tesla Supercharger, and exit 309 for Kettleman City lists Tesla Supercharger, EV Range and Electrify America.

Where can I find food and a motel on I-5 near Kettleman City?

Exit 309 for CA 41 lists 16 food places, including Baja Fresh, Bravo Farms and Denny's, and two lodging places, Best Western and Quality Inn. The data notes Kettleman City is near the halfway point between Los Angeles and San Francisco or Sacramento.

What exit is Lost Hills on I-5?

Lost Hills is at exit 278, CA 46, which also serves Paso Robles and Wasco. The exit lists a Lost Hills Travel Center, 14 gas stations, 11 food places and two lodging places as mapped.
