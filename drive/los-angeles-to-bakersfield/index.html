Los Angeles to Bakersfield on I-5 over the Grapevine

This guide runs I-5 north from Boyle Heights at exit 135 over Newhall Pass, the Five Mile Grade, Tejon Pass and Grapevine Canyon to the San Joaquin Valley and exit 257 near Bakersfield. Landmarks are listed along the way, from Yaanga and Beale's Cut to Six Flags Magic Mountain and the Kern County orchards. Gas, EV charging, food and lodging are as mapped in OpenStreetMap, not live.

Miles 135 to 140Boyle Heights to Elysian Park

The freeway threads the East Los Angeles Interchange in Boyle Heights, runs through Lincoln Heights, the city's first suburb, and crosses the Los Angeles River where the Arroyo Seco joins it, beside the wooded hills of Elysian Park.

  1. Exit 135 · on your left going north · History

    Yaanga and the Tongva

    Across the river, where downtown Los Angeles now stands, was Yaanga, recorded as the largest and most influential Tongva village in the region. Full story

  2. Exit 137 · History

    Arroyo Seco Parkway

    The freeway crosses the Los Angeles River here, where the Arroyo Seco flows in, and meets the Arroyo Seco Parkway, one of the oldest freeways in the United States. Full story

  3. Exit 138 · on your left going north · History

    Elysian Park and Chavez Ravine

    The wooded hills beside the freeway are Elysian Park, created in 1886 and, at 600 acres, one of the largest parks in Los Angeles. Full story

Miles 140 to 145LA River and Griffith Park

The freeway follows the Los Angeles River through the Glendale Narrows, with the brushy hills of Griffith Park on the west side.

  1. Exit 141 · Land & water

    Los Angeles River

    The channel beside the freeway is the Los Angeles River. Full story

  2. Exit 141 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Griffith Park

    The brushy hills west of the freeway are Griffith Park, more than 4,000 acres of chaparral and parkland that the city calls the largest urban-wilderness municipal park in the United States. Full story

  3. Exit 144 · on your left going north · History

    The 1933 Griffith Park fire

    On October 3, 1933, a brush fire in Griffith Park killed at least 29 men who volunteered or were ordered to fight it. Full story

  4. Exit 144 · Good stops

    Zoo, Autry and Travel Town

    Three family stops sit along the north side of Griffith Park: the Los Angeles Zoo, the Autry Museum of the American West, and Travel Town, a railroad museum with 43 full-size engines, cars and other rolling stock. Full story

  5. Exit 145 · on your right going north · History

    Grand Central Air Terminal

    Just east of the freeway stands the 1928 terminal building of Grand Central Airport, once a major gateway to Los Angeles by air. Full story

Miles 145 to 153Burbank and Sun Valley

Studios, the airport and the neighborhoods of Glendale, Burbank and Sun Valley spread across the flat east end of the San Fernando Valley, below the rocky Verdugo Mountains.

  1. Exit 146 · on your right going north · History

    Verdugo Mountains

    The rocky range rising east of the freeway is the Verdugo Mountains, topping out at 3,126 feet. Full story

  2. Exit 147 · Towns

    Burbank, the Media Capital

    Burbank, which the freeway passes through, is commonly known as the Media Capital of the World. Full story

  3. Exit 149 · on your left going north · History

    Lockheed's hidden factory

    During World War II, Lockheed's aircraft factory beside the Burbank airport was disguised from the air. Full story

  4. Exit 150 · on your left going north · Towns

    Sun Valley's thorny name

    Long before it was Sun Valley, this was Wixánga, a Fernandeño Tongva village whose name means something like place of the thorns, for the prickly pear cactus growing here. Full story

  5. Exit 152 · Orchards & farms

    When the valley grew wheat

    In 1874, J. B. Lankershim and Isaac Van Nuys began dry-farming wheat across the southern half of the San Fernando Valley. Full story

Miles 153 to 162North San Fernando Valley

Pacoima, Mission Hills and Sylmar fill the north end of the valley, once a land of mission vineyards, wheat and olive groves, with the San Gabriel Mountains rising close by.

  1. Exit 155 · on your right going north · Towns

    Pacoima, Ritchie Valens' hometown

    Pacoima takes its name from Pakoinga, the name of a Native village here meaning entrance; Spanish ears heard its ng sound as an m. It is also the hometown of Ritchie Valens, born Richard Valenzuela here in 1941, who turned the Mexican folk song La Bamba into a rock and roll hit in 1958. Full story

  2. Exit 158 · on your left going north · History

    The dam that almost failed

    The reservoirs just west of the freeway once supplied about 80 percent of Los Angeles' water. Full story

  3. Exit 159 · Orchards & farms

    Sylmar's sea of olives

    Sylmar's name, adopted in 1893, joins Latin words for forest and sea, for the sea of olive trees that covered these slopes. Full story

  4. Exit 159 · on your right going north · Oddities

    Power from the Columbia River

    Near the freeway sits the Sylmar Converter Station, the south end of the Pacific DC Intertie, a power line that runs 846 miles from the Columbia River near The Dalles, Oregon. Full story

  5. Exit 161 · on your right going north · History

    Los Angeles Aqueduct Cascades

    On the hillside beside the freeway, water from the eastern Sierra Nevada tumbles down the Cascades, the end of the Los Angeles Aqueduct. Full story

Miles 162 to 168Newhall Pass and Newhall

Newhall Pass, between the San Gabriel and Santa Susana Mountains, links the San Fernando Valley with the Santa Clarita Valley, where Newhall grew up as a railroad and oil town.

  1. Exit 162 · History

    Newhall Pass Interchange

    This tangle of ramps, where I-5 meets the Antelope Valley Freeway, is the Newhall Pass interchange, and parts of I-5 here are up to 21 lanes wide. Full story

  2. Exit 162 · on your right going north · History

    Oak of the Golden Dream

    In Placerita Canyon, a few miles east, Francisco López made what the state's landmark plaque calls California's first authenticated gold discovery, on March 9, 1842. Full story

  3. Exit 163 · on your right going north · History

    Beale's Cut

    Before tunnels and freeways, stagecoaches crossed this pass through Beale's Cut, a slot dug through the mountain with picks and shovels and eventually deepened to 90 feet. Full story

  4. Exit 167 · on your right going north · Good stops

    William S. Hart Park

    In Newhall, the ranch of silent film cowboy William S. Hart is now a 265-acre park with his Spanish-style mansion, trails and a barnyard of farm animals. Full story

  5. Exit 167 · on your left going north · History

    Pico Number 4 oil well

    Up Pico Canyon, west of the freeway, Well Number 4 struck oil on September 26, 1876, and became the first commercially successful oil well in the western United States. Full story

Miles 168 to 177Santa Clarita Valley

Planned neighborhoods of Valencia spread along the Santa Clara River, with Magic Mountain's roller coasters on the hills west of the freeway.

  1. Exit 169 · History

    Tataviam homeland

    The Santa Clarita Valley is considered the heart of the homeland of the Tataviam people, a name that means people on the south slope in the neighboring Kitanemuk language. Full story

  2. Exit 170 · Land & water

    Santa Clara River

    The Santa Clara River, which passes under the freeway near Magic Mountain Parkway, runs 83 miles to the Pacific and is one of the least altered rivers in Southern California. Full story

  3. Exit 170 · on your left going north · Oddities

    Six Flags Magic Mountain

    The roller coaster tracks twisting over the hills west of the freeway belong to Six Flags Magic Mountain, which opened on May 29, 1971. Full story

  4. Exit 172 · on your left going north · History

    The Death Valley rescue

    Near where Highway 126 meets the freeway stood the adobe headquarters of Rancho San Francisco, first built about 1804 as a granary for Mission San Fernando. Full story

  5. Exit 172 · on your right going north · History

    St. Francis Dam disaster

    Just before midnight on March 12, 1928, the St. Francis Dam failed in San Francisquito Canyon, about 10 miles north of Santa Clarita. Full story

  6. Exit 173 · on your left going north · History

    Val Verde, the Black Palm Springs

    In the hills west of the freeway, Black investors led by Sidney Dones founded a resort community in 1924, when African Americans were often barred from public beaches and pools. Full story

Miles 177 to 191Castaic and the Five Mile Grade

North of Castaic, the long Five Mile Grade leads into the Angeles National Forest, where the freeway crosses the Sierra Pelona Mountains between Castaic Lake and Pyramid Lake.

  1. Exit 176 · Oddities

    Five Mile Grade crossover

    On the Five Mile Grade north of Castaic, the two sides of the freeway trade places, so for about 5 miles traffic keeps to the left. Full story

  2. Exit 176 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Castaic Lake and its name

    Castaic takes its name from the Chumash word Kaštiq, meaning the eye. Full story

  3. Exit 183 · History

    The old Ridge Route

    East of the freeway, the original Ridge Route of 1915 winds along the ridgetops from Castaic toward Gorman. Full story

  4. Exit 191 · on your left going north · History

    Highway 99 under the lake

    Beneath Pyramid Lake lies a drowned highway. Full story

  5. Exit 191 · on your left going north · Good stops

    Vista del Lago

    The Vista del Lago Visitors Center, off its own exit, sits above Pyramid Lake with an outdoor overlook and exhibits from the California Department of Water Resources. Full story

Miles 191 to 203Pyramid Lake to Gorman

Pyramid Lake fills the canyon of Piru Creek west of the freeway in the Sierra Pelona Mountains, and farther north the land opens into Peace Valley around Gorman.

  1. Exit 198 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Quail Lake on the fault

    Quail Lake, along Highway 138 east of the freeway, lies in the rift zone of the San Andreas Fault and stores water for the West Branch of the California Aqueduct. Full story

  2. Exit 202 · Towns

    Gorman and its wildflowers

    In spring after a wet winter, California poppies and lupine can blanket the hills around Gorman. Full story

Miles 203 to 210Tejon Pass and Lebec

Tejon Pass sits among the open hills of Tejon Ranch around Lebec.

  1. Exit 204 · Land & water

    Tejon Pass summit

    Near the top of Tejon Pass, I-5 reaches 4,144 feet, its highest point in California, close to the line between Los Angeles and Kern counties. Full story

  2. Exit 205 · on your left going north · History

    The Brink's jewelry heist

    At 2 in the morning on July 11, 2022, a Brink's tractor-trailer carrying jewelry from a show in San Mateo to one in Pasadena pulled into a truck stop at the Frazier Mountain Park Road exit. Full story

  3. Exit 205 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Frazier Mountain

    The broad, pine-covered peak west of the pass is Frazier Mountain, 8,017 feet high. Full story

  4. Exit 207 · Orchards & farms

    Tejon Ranch cattle

    The open hills around Lebec are part of Tejon Ranch, more than 270,000 acres and the largest single piece of private land in California. Full story

  5. Exit 207 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Castac Lake, the eye

    East of Lebec lies Castac Lake, a natural salty lake that formed about 10,000 years ago when the fan of sediment washed down by Cuddy Creek dammed a valley along the Garlock Fault. Full story

Miles 210 to 215Grapevine Canyon

Grapevine Canyon carries the freeway's long, steep grade past Fort Tejon's centuries-old valley oaks to the southern edge of the San Joaquin Valley.

  1. Exit 210 · on your left going north · History

    Fort Tejon

    The adobe buildings among the oaks beside the freeway are Fort Tejon, an Army post from 1854 to 1864 on the main route between the Central Valley and Southern California. Full story

  2. Exit 210 · on your left going north · Oddities

    Peter Lebeck's oak

    On the fort grounds is the grave of Peter Lebeck, buried under a valley oak in 1837. Full story

  3. Exit 210 · Land & water

    The Grapevine grade

    This steep stretch through Grapevine Canyon is the Grapevine itself, a 6 percent grade that runs 5 miles and drops about 1,600 feet. Full story

  4. Exit 215 · History

    California's first reservation

    These mountains are ancestral homeland of the Tejon Indian Tribe, a federally recognized tribe of Kitanemuk, Yokuts, Paiute and Chumash people based in Bakersfield. Full story

Miles 215 to 221Wheeler Ridge and the valley's edge

Below the Grapevine the land flattens at Wheeler Ridge, where the Tehachapi and San Emigdio mountains meet the floor of the San Joaquin Valley and Highway 99 meets I-5.

  1. Exit 215 · on your right going north · Oddities

    Water lifted over the mountains

    About 6 miles east of here, the Edmonston Pumping Plant lifts California Aqueduct water 1,926 feet up and over the Tehachapi Mountains toward Southern California. Full story

  2. Exit 215 · Land & water

    Where the valley begins

    At the foot of the Grapevine, the freeway reaches the southern tip of the San Joaquin Valley, about 1,500 feet above sea level. Full story

  3. Exit 219 · Land & water

    Wheeler Ridge, a growing fold

    The long, low ridge where the mountains meet the valley floor here is Wheeler Ridge, a fold in the earth's crust that is still growing. Full story

  4. Exit 221 · Land & water

    The 1952 earthquake

    At 4:52 in the morning on July 21, 1952, the White Wolf Fault broke near Wheeler Ridge in a magnitude 7.3 earthquake, the strongest in California since 1906. Full story

Miles 221 to 234The vanished southern lakes

Flat farmland covers the old beds of Kern Lake and Buena Vista Lake, with the San Emigdio Mountains forming the valley's southern wall and the oil country of Maricopa and Taft off to the west.

  1. Exit 221 · History

    Start of the West Side Freeway

    Highway 99 and I-5 meet at the Wheeler Ridge interchange. Full story

  2. Exit 225 · on your right going north · History

    James Dean's speeding ticket

    On September 30, 1955, the actor James Dean was driving north on Highway 99, on his way to a sports car race in Salinas, when a Highway Patrol officer pulled him over near here and wrote him a speeding ticket. Full story

  3. Exit 225 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Wind Wolves Preserve

    In the hills southwest of here lies Wind Wolves Preserve, 93,000 acres of grassland, oak and canyon that its owner, The Wildlands Conservancy, calls the largest nonprofit nature preserve on the West Coast. Full story

  4. Exit 225 · on your left going north · History

    The Lakeview Gusher

    Near Maricopa, in March 1910, an oil well called Lakeview Number One blew out and would not stop. Full story

  5. Exit 228 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Mountains named for a saint

    The mountains forming the valley's southern wall are the San Emigdio Mountains, which top out at 7,492 feet. Full story

  6. Exit 230 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Kern Lake's empty bed

    The flat fields near the freeway here were once Kern Lake, the smallest of three big lakes at the south end of the valley. Full story

Miles 234 to 246Kern County orchards

Kern County's valley floor here is farm country, where almonds and pistachios are among the most valuable crops, and Highways 119 and 43 lead west to the oil towns of Taft and Maricopa.

  1. Exit 239 · Orchards & farms

    Kern County nut country

    Many of the neat rows of trees in Kern County are almonds and pistachios. Full story

  2. Exit 244 · on your left going north · Towns

    Taft, once called Moron

    Highway 119 leads to Taft, an oil town at the valley's southwest edge. Full story

  3. Exit 244 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Buena Vista Lake

    Southwest of here, much of the farmland was once Buena Vista Lake, fed by the Kern River and the second largest of the lakes in the Tulare Basin. Full story

Miles 246 to 257Kern River and the Elk Hills

The freeway crosses the Kern River near the Kern Water Bank's recharge basins and runs past the low, bare Elk Hills.

  1. Exit 246 · Land & water

    Crossing the Kern River

    The freeway crosses the Kern River here, though this far downstream its channel is often dry. Full story

  2. Exit 246 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Kern Water Bank

    Near here, the Kern Water Bank covers about 32 square miles of the southern San Joaquin Valley. Full story

  3. Exit 253 · on your left going north · Good stops

    Tule Elk State Natural Reserve

    A few miles southwest, Tule Elk State Natural Reserve keeps a herd of about 30 to 35 tule elk on nearly 1,000 acres, watched from a viewing platform. Full story

  4. Exit 253 · on your left going north · History

    Elk Hills and Teapot Dome

    The low hills to the west are the Elk Hills, over one of the most productive oil fields in the country. Full story

  5. Exit 257 · History

    Henry Miller, the Cattle King

    Buttonwillow was a headquarters of Miller and Lux, the company of Henry Miller, known as the Cattle King of California. Full story

Questions drivers ask

Where is the rest area on I-5 between Los Angeles and Bakersfield?

The Tejon Pass Rest Area is listed at mile 206.1 and serves both directions.

Where can I get gas on I-5 between Los Angeles and Bakersfield?

Gas is mapped at 47 exits in this stretch, including exit 167 at Lyons Avenue, exit 176 at Castaic, exit 202 at Gorman and exit 253 at Stockdale Highway. Listings come from OpenStreetMap and are not live.

Where are EV chargers on I-5 between Los Angeles and Bakersfield?

EV chargers are mapped at 20 exits in this range, including a Tesla Supercharger at exit 205 for Frazier Mountain Park Road and at exit 219B for Laval Road West. Charger counts and speeds are not in the data, and listings are not live.

What is Grapevine Canyon on I-5?

Grapevine Canyon, around miles 210 to 215, carries the freeway's long, steep grade past Fort Tejon's centuries-old valley oaks down to the southern edge of the San Joaquin Valley.

What exit is Gorman on I-5?

Gorman is exit 202. Gas, food and lodging are mapped there, and in spring after a wet winter poppies and lupine can cover the nearby hills.
