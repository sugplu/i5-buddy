Thanksgiving 2026 on I-5 in California

Thanksgiving falls on Thursday, November 26, 2026 (California Energy Commission, State Holiday – Thanksgiving Day 2026). Many people will drive the long way home on I-5. This page covers the official holiday notices, the Grapevine, chains and storms, where to stop by exit, and the run north to Oregon. Road conditions change by the hour, so it doesn't say whether anything is open now. For that, use the live page at Is the Grapevine open?, which shows CHP and Caltrans feeds refreshed every 2 minutes, and Caltrans QuickMap.

When to leave

What is official so far. Every Thanksgiving the CHP runs a Holiday Enforcement Period, when more officers are on the road; its General Order lists Thanksgiving among the holidays that get one and says the CHP picks the dates each year (CHP General Order 100.90, revised October 2025). We have not found the 2026 Thanksgiving dates on the CHP site yet, so this page does not state them. Check CHP News for the 2026 notice.

For reference only: last year the CHP's notice (published November 24, 2025) said the period began at 6:01 p.m. on Wednesday, November 26, 2025 and ran through 11:59 p.m. on Sunday, November 30, 2025 (CHP, Thanksgiving safety reminder, 2025). Those are 2025 dates and may not match 2026.

Lane closures. We have not found a 2026 Caltrans notice about lifting lane closures for the holiday either. Caltrans planned lane closures and Caltrans QuickMap show what is scheduled and what is happening now.

Tips that hold in any year:

  • Open QuickMap before you leave, and again before the mountain passes.
  • Fill up when you have the chance. The exits in the Where to stop section below list the fuel, EV and food the data has for each.
  • Rest areas are far apart in places, so look at the rest area table below before you start.

The Grapevine (Tejon Pass)

The Grapevine is the climb between the Los Angeles basin and the San Joaquin Valley. The climb and descent run from about mile 191 near Pyramid Lake to mile 221 at Wheeler Ridge.

  • Pyramid Lake to Gorman (miles 191 to 203): Pyramid Lake sits in the canyon west of the freeway. Farther north the land opens into Peace Valley around Gorman (exit 202). Quail Lake, near exit 198 along Highway 138, lies in the rift zone of the San Andreas Fault.
  • The summit (exit 204): Near the top of Tejon Pass, I-5 reaches 4,144 feet. This is its highest point in California, close to the Los Angeles–Kern county line.
  • Tejon Pass and Lebec (miles 203 to 210): Open hills belonging to Tejon Ranch. Frazier Mountain, 8,017 feet, is the pine-covered peak west of the pass.
  • The grade (exits 210 to 215): Through Grapevine Canyon, the road drops about 1,600 feet over 5 miles on a 6 percent grade. Fort Tejon's adobe buildings sit among the oaks at exit 210.
  • The valley floor (exits 215 to 221): The land flattens at Wheeler Ridge. Highway 99 meets I-5 at the Wheeler Ridge interchange (exit 221).

Chains and storms

Late November can bring the first snow to the high passes. When Caltrans posts chain controls, the level tells you who needs chains. In Caltrans' own wording, put simply (QuickMap FAQ):

  • R-0: No chain controls right now.
  • R-1: Chains are required on all vehicles except passenger cars and light trucks under 6,000 pounds gross weight that have snow tires on at least two drive wheels. If you use snow tires, you still have to carry chains. Any vehicle towing a trailer needs chains on one drive axle, and trailers with brakes need chains on at least one axle.
  • R-2: Chains or traction devices are required on all vehicles except four-wheel or all-wheel drive vehicles with snow-tread tires on all four. Those vehicles must still carry traction devices.
  • R-3: Chains or traction devices are required on every vehicle, with no exceptions.
  • ESC: The CHP is escorting all traffic because of weather.
  • TH: Truck-trailer combinations are being held at the chain control point. (Caltrans chain control status chart)

Caltrans QuickMap shows the chain controls in effect on the map.

When snow and ice close the freeway over Tejon Pass, the closure usually lasts four to six hours, and some last longer than a day.

North to the Oregon line (the Siskiyous)

North of Redding, I-5 climbs into volcano and mountain country. Snow is common here in winter; check QuickMap before you go.

  • Mount Shasta and Black Butte (miles 735 to 746): The canyon opens at the foot of Mount Shasta, 14,179 feet. Black Butte, a dark cluster of lava domes, sits close beside the freeway near exit 742. Black Butte Summit is on this stretch. Highway 89 leaves at exit 736 for McCloud. Mount Shasta City is at exit 738.
  • Weed (miles 746 to 756): An old lumber town, named for Abner Weed, who found that the strong winds here were good for drying lumber. Weed Airport Rest Area is at mile 753.12, in both directions.
  • Shasta Valley (miles 756 to 770): A wide, dry valley of hay fields, cattle and juniper. Winters are cool to cold, with fairly frequent snow.
  • Yreka (miles 770 to 785): Gold Rush country. Yreka averages only about 12 inches of snow a year, mostly from November through February. An unnamed rest area is at mile 779.69, southbound.
  • Klamath River to the Oregon line (miles 785 to 797): The freeway crosses the Klamath River near exit 786. Randolph E. Collier Rest Area is at mile 785.79, southbound. Hilt, at exit 796, is the northernmost I-5 exit in California. Just over the Oregon line, Siskiyou Summit usually gets snow from November through April.
  • Coming south into California: Traffic entering the state on I-5 stops at the agricultural inspection station near Hornbrook (exit 790).

Our data lists no fuel, EV or food by name for this stretch, so plan your fill-ups farther south. For live conditions, see the Siskiyous on the live page and QuickMap.
