The Sacramento River canyon on I-5

From about exit 688 to 740, I-5 crosses Shasta Lake, follows the steep forested Sacramento River canyon, passes the granite spires of Castle Crags and Dunsmuir, and opens onto the foot of Mount Shasta. I-5 Buddy, a talking GPS guide to Interstate 5 in California, tells the story of this stretch as you drive it. Gas, EV, food and lodging are as mapped in OpenStreetMap, not live.

Miles 688 to 704Shasta Lake

The freeway crosses Shasta Lake, whose steep shores are covered with tall evergreens and manzanita.

  1. Exit 688 · Oddities

    The drowned highway

    Before the lake, old Highway 99 ran through Mountain Gate and then plunged into the valley that Shasta Lake now fills. Full story

  2. Exit 690 · History

    Bass Hill stage road

    Near the Bridge Bay exit stands a marker to the stagecoach days. Full story

  3. Exit 689 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Shasta Lake

    Shasta Lake is California's largest reservoir, with 365 miles of steep, wooded shoreline. Full story

  4. Exit 691 · History

    Pit River Bridge

    The Pit River Bridge is a double-decker. Full story

  5. Exit 693 · Oddities

    The giant median

    Just north of the Pit River Bridge, the two directions of I-5 split apart, leaving what is said to be the second-largest median in California. Full story

  6. Exit 694 · Land & water

    Shasta salamanders

    The limestone hills around this lake hide salamanders found nowhere else on Earth. Full story

  7. Exit 695 · on your right going north · Good stops

    Lake Shasta Caverns

    Getting to these limestone caves takes a catamaran ride across the lake's McCloud arm and then a bus ride up a steep mountain road. Full story

  8. Exit 696 · History

    Winnemem Wintu homeland

    The McCloud River arm of this lake is the homeland of the Winnemem Wintu, whose name means middle water people or middle river people. Full story

  9. Exit 698 · on your right going north · History

    The Salt Fire

    On June 30, 2021, a fire started beside I-5 near the Salt Creek exit and shut down the freeway. Full story

  10. Exit 700 · History

    The first federal fish hatchery

    In 1872, the nation's first federal fish hatchery opened on the McCloud River, which now flows into this lake. Full story

  11. Exit 702 · on your left going north · Towns

    Lakehead, top of the lake

    Lakehead sits on the northern shore of Shasta Lake. Full story

Miles 704 to 722Sacramento River canyon

The freeway follows a steep, forested canyon, past old stage stops and Gold Rush camps.

  1. Exit 707 · History

    Delta, end of the line

    When the railroad pushed up this canyon in the 1880s, the town of Delta was the end of the line for about two years. Full story

  2. Exit 711 · Land & water

    The upper Sacramento River

    The river beside the freeway is the upper Sacramento. Full story

  3. Exit 712 · History

    Portuguese Flat

    Near the Pollard Flat exit was Portuguese Flat, a Gold Rush camp of the early 1850s made up largely of Portuguese miners. Full story

  4. Exit 715 · History

    River, rails and road

    The river, the railroad and the freeway all squeeze through this one canyon. Full story

  5. Exit 718 · History

    Southern's stage station

    The name of this exit goes back to Simeon Southern, nicknamed Sims, who opened a log stage station and hotel here in 1859, where travelers stopped to rest and eat for 50 years. Full story

  6. Exit 720 · History

    The Siskiyou Trail

    Long before this was a freeway, it was a web of Native American footpaths between California and the Northwest. Full story

Miles 722 to 735Castle Crags and Dunsmuir

Granite spires rise west of the freeway, and an old railroad town fills the canyon bottom. Dunsmuir gets about 63 inches of rain and snow a year, about three and a half times as much as Yreka or Medford.

  1. Exit 723 · on your left going north · History

    Homeland and the 1855 battle

    The country around Castle Crags is the ancestral home of the Okwanuchu Shasta people. Full story

  2. Exit 724 · Good stops

    Castle Crags State Park

    In the 1890s, mineral water from a spring here was bottled and shipped all over the world. Full story

  3. Exit 726 · on your left going north · Land & water

    Castle Crags

    These granite spires rise from about 2,000 feet at the river to more than 6,500 feet at the top. Full story

  4. Exit 728 · on your left going north · Good stops

    Sleep in a caboose

    Just off this exit, a lodge founded in 1968 lets guests sleep in old cabooses and eat in a restaurant inside an original train car, with Castle Crags towering above. Full story

  5. Exit 729 · Towns

    Dunsmuir, the railroad town

    Dunsmuir was a railroad town first, nicknamed Pusher, because extra steam engines were added here to push trains up the steep grades north of town. Full story

  6. Exit 730 · History

    Upper Soda Springs

    Under the freeway in north Dunsmuir lies Upper Soda Springs, now a riverside park. Full story

  7. Exit 731 · Oddities

    Babe Ruth in Dunsmuir

    In 1924, New York Yankees stars Babe Ruth and Bob Meusel came to Dunsmuir on a barnstorming tour and played an exhibition game against the locals in the city park. Full story

  8. Exit 732 · Good stops

    Hedge Creek Falls

    A short trail from this exit leads to a waterfall with a small cave behind it, so you can walk behind the falling water. Full story

  9. Exit 733 · on your left going north · Oddities

    Where Shasta soda began

    Just north of Dunsmuir was Shasta Springs, a summer resort on the railroad main line in the late 1800s and early 1900s. Full story

  10. Exit 734 · on your left going north · History

    The Cantara Loop spill

    On the night of July 14, 1991, a train derailed at a horseshoe curve called the Cantara Loop, upstream from Dunsmuir, and a tank car spilled about 19,000 gallons of pesticide into the river. Full story

Miles 735 to 740Mount Shasta and Black Butte

The canyon opens onto the foot of the volcano, with the city of Mount Shasta, the dark cone of Black Butte and Black Butte Summit.

  1. Exit 736 · on your right going north · Towns

    McCloud, a company town

    Highway 89 heads east to McCloud, a lumber company town on the southern slope of Mount Shasta. Full story

  2. Exit 737 · on your left going north · Orchards & farms

    Mount Shasta trout hatchery

    On Old Stage Road in Mount Shasta, the Mount Shasta Hatchery has raised trout since 1888, and the state calls it the oldest trout hatchery west of the Mississippi. Full story

  3. Exit 738 · on your right going north · Towns

    Mount Shasta City

    This town was called Strawberry Valley, then Berryvale, then Sisson, after Justin Sisson, who gave the land for the railroad station. Full story

  4. Exit 739 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Headwaters of the Sacramento

    In Mount Shasta City Park, water pours straight out of a hillside at Big Springs. Full story

  5. Exit 740 · on your right going north · Land & water

    Mount Shasta

    Mount Shasta rises to 14,179 feet. Full story

Questions drivers ask

What exit is Shasta Lake on I-5?

Shasta Lake is at exit 689, on the left going north. The lake is California's largest reservoir, with 365 miles of steep, wooded shoreline.

Where can I get gas on I-5 through the Sacramento River canyon?

Mapped gas in this stretch includes Shell at exit 702 (Lakeshore Drive), Pollard Flat at exit 712, Chevron at exit 724 (Castella) and exit 730 (Central Dunsmuir), Valero at exit 729, and 76, Chevron and Valero at exit 738 (Central Mount Shasta). These are listed in the data, not live, so open hours and prices are not shown.

Are there EV chargers on I-5 between Shasta Lake and Mount Shasta?

ChargePoint is mapped at exit 702 (Lakeshore Drive) and exit 730 (Central Dunsmuir). Exit 738 (Central Mount Shasta) lists ChargePoint, Tesla Supercharger, Rivian Adventure Network and Rivian Waypoints, all as mapped, not live.

Where are the rest areas on I-5 near Shasta Lake?

O'Brien Rest Area is at mile 692.9 and Lakehead Rest Area is at mile 704.7. The data does not say which direction each one serves.

Where can I eat or stay in Dunsmuir on I-5?

Exit 730 (Central Dunsmuir) lists seven mapped food places, including Burger Barn, Cafe Maddalena and Pizza Factory. Exit 732 lists eight mapped lodging places, including Cedar Lodge Motel and Travelodge, and exit 728 lists the Jubilee Railroad Wilderness Lodge with its dining car.
