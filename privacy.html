Privacy

What leaves your phone, what stays on it, and your choices.

Effective November 21, 2026 · Version 2026-11-21.2

I-5 Buddy is made by Dominares. Dominares Tools is run by Joseph Linares, a sole proprietor based in San Diego, California. "We" on this page means Dominares (Dominares Tools). For questions or requests, write to support@dominares.org.

This notice covers the I-5 Buddy app at i5.dominares.org, the I-5 Buddy iPhone app, the I-5 Buddy Android app (in a closed test on Google Play, not public yet), and copies of it still running from its old address, sugplu.github.io/i5-buddy. The dominares.org website has its own privacy policy.

The short version

  • No account and no sign-in.
  • No ads, no analytics or tracking scripts, and no cookies: I-5 Buddy sets none.
  • We never sell your information or share it for advertising.
  • Your exact GPS position stays on your phone, with one exception: when you ask for directions, your position goes to our server as the route's starting point (see Directions). I-5 Buddy never saves it on a server.
  • Some features tell other services roughly where you are: Live road info, Directions, the 3D map, the narration voice's recordings and online narration voices. They ask about the part of I-5 around you or ahead of you, or the places you search for. Live road info is off until you turn it on, and Directions sends nothing until you search for a place and say it may.
  • Your trips, stamps and settings are saved only on your phone. We can't see them.
  • If you send feedback, we get your message, your email, your name if you give one, the app version and your browser type. We use them only to read your message and reply.

What leaves your phone, and why

Here is everything I-5 Buddy sends, feature by feature. Most of it happens only after you turn something on or tap something.

Opening the app

Every time

Your browser loads the app, its road data and the landmark photos from i5.dominares.org. That address runs on Cloudflare, which gets the files from GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc.), where the app is published. Like any website, Cloudflare sees standard web request data: your IP address, your browser and device type, your language and which file was asked for. GitHub only gets Cloudflare's request for each file, not your IP address, browser or language. Our server stores no log of visits (that log is switched off), and Cloudflare's dashboard shows us traffic totals.

The old address works differently. Links to sugplu.github.io/i5-buddy reach GitHub Pages directly, and the page then sends you on to i5.dominares.org. Home Screen apps added from sugplu.github.io keep loading from GitHub Pages, so GitHub sees those requests directly. They show a note asking you to open I-5 Buddy at i5.dominares.org and add it to your Home Screen again.

In Safari on an iPhone or iPad, the page also asks Safari to show its banner for the I-5 Buddy iPhone app. Safari gets what the banner shows from Apple's App Store, and Apple's privacy policy applies.

The iPhone app has these files built in, so opening it loads nothing from i5.dominares.org or GitHub. Its other features work as described below.

In the Android app

If you installed I-5 Buddy from Google Play

The Android app is this same web app. It opens i5.dominares.org in your phone's browser, usually Chrome, without the address bar. So it loads, saves and sends what this notice describes for the website, and nothing else. The app adds no analytics, ads, account or notifications of its own.

Location. Your browser asks whether i5.dominares.org may use your location, and Android may ask whether the browser itself may use location. You can change your answer later in the browser's settings (in Chrome: Settings, Site settings, Location). The app uses location only while it's open, never in the background. Android's location service works out your position; on most phones that service is part of Google Play services, which may contact Google under Google's own policy. Your browser hands the position to the page on your phone, and I-5 Buddy never sends it anywhere.

What it saves. Your settings, passport and trips are kept in your browser's storage for i5.dominares.org, the same place the website uses in that browser. So uninstalling the app may not erase them. To erase them, long-press the I-5 Buddy icon and tap Site Settings, or delete the site data for i5.dominares.org in your browser's settings (in Chrome: Settings, Site settings, All sites).

Google Play. Google delivers the app and its updates under Google's own privacy policy. Google Play shows us statistics, such as how many people installed the app, and crash reports from phones whose owners share usage and diagnostics with Google, without names. If you rate or review the app, Google shows your review publicly under your Google Account name. If you join our test before the app is public, we see the email address you join with, and we use it only to run the test.

Send feedback

Only when you tap Send

Settings > About > Send feedback sends us:

  • your name, if you give one
  • your email address, so we can reply
  • your message
  • the app version, like 2.5.1

Your browser also sends its user agent: a line of text naming your browser, its version and your device's system. We store the first 200 characters of it with your message, along with the time your message arrived. The app adds nothing else: no location, trips or settings.

Your message is stored in a Cloudflare database run for Dominares, and we read it in our Dominares console. We use what you send only to read your message and reply to you.

Your IP address is not stored. To stop spam, we take at most 5 messages an hour from one internet address. To count them, we keep a scrambled code made from your IP address and a random value that changes every day (a salted hash). We keep it with the time your message arrived. Someone with access to the database could work an IP address back out of that code, so we treat it as personal information. Each code and each day's random value is kept at least two days, and is deleted when we next accept a message after that. If nobody writes for a while, they stay until someone does. Cloudflare's automatic database history can still bring them back for up to 30 days after they're deleted.

Live road info

Only after you turn it on

It starts off. You can switch it on from the card on the Drive tab or in Settings > Live road info, and switch it off there any time. While it's on and the app is open and online:

  • Traffic. Every 2 minutes the app asks i5.dominares.org for the list of crashes and closures on all of I-5 in California. Our server gets that list from the California Highway Patrol and Caltrans. The request carries no location: everyone gets the same list, and your phone picks out what's ahead of you. Our server keeps nothing about who asked.
  • Weather. Your phone asks the National Weather Service (api.weather.gov) for forecasts and alerts at points on I-5 about 10 to 120 miles ahead of you.
  • Chain controls and freeway signs. The app asks i5.dominares.org for one list of the chain controls and freeway signs on all of I-5 in California. Our server gets it from Caltrans. Like traffic, the request carries no location, and our server keeps nothing about who asked. If our server doesn't answer, your phone loads them from Caltrans (cwwp2.dot.ca.gov) for the Caltrans districts ahead of you.
  • Cameras. The Drive tab loads pictures from the 3 nearest Caltrans cameras ahead, and from any camera you open, about once a minute while they're on screen.

The weather points are counted from the nearest 5-mile mark on I-5, not from your exact spot. So weather.gov can tell only roughly where you are (within a few miles) and which way you're going. The app checks about every 5 miles or 10 minutes. The cameras you load show Caltrans which stretch of I-5 you're on, often to within a mile. As with any web request, these services also get your IP address and can tell the request came from I-5 Buddy. When you reopen the app, it uses the last spot it saved, even with GPS off.

Directions (Where to?)

Only when you search for a place or ask for a route, after you say it may

The app first looks through its own places on I-5 (cities, landmarks, missions, rest areas, exits and the stops at them), on your phone. The first time you search, it tells you what it sends and asks first. If you say Not now, it keeps to its own places and sends nothing; the countdown to an I-5 city still works.

When you say it may, the app sends these to our server at i5.dominares.org (Cloudflare):

  • Searching: the words you type, and your position rounded to about 100 meters, so the closest places come first.
  • A route: your position as the starting point, and the destination's position. While you drive a route, the app follows it on your phone; it asks for a new route only when you leave the planned one.

Our server asks Geoapify, our map data provider (its data comes from OpenStreetMap), for the places or the route, and sends the answer back. Geoapify gets the request from our server, not from your phone, so it doesn't see your IP address. Our server keeps no log of searches or routes. To answer the same question faster, Cloudflare keeps a copy of each answer at the edge, labeled with the question: search answers for up to a day, for an area of about 5 kilometers, and routes for up to 10 minutes. Those copies hold no IP address and nothing that says who asked.

Your recent destinations, and the places you save as Home and Work, are kept only on your phone.

Maps

The 3D map: only after you tap 3D

The regular map is drawn on your phone from the app's own data, so it loads no map pictures. A camera picture loads from Caltrans only when you tap a camera on it.

While you look at a route or drive one, Directions shows a map from OpenFreeMap the same way.

The 3D map loads only after you tap 3D on the Map tab. Your phone then loads map pictures from OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org) and hill shapes from Amazon Web Services (s3.amazonaws.com). As with any web request, they get your IP address and can tell the request came from I-5 Buddy. The pictures cover the area on screen, which starts at your spot on I-5 and follows you. So these services can see roughly where you are. It stops when you tap 3D again or leave the Map tab.

Voice commands

Only after you tap a microphone button

Voice commands start when you tap the microphone at the top, or Answer by voice in trivia. I-5 Buddy then uses your browser's own speech recognition to turn what you say into text. To do that, your browser may send the sound to its maker's speech service: Google for Chrome, Apple (Siri and Dictation) for Safari, Microsoft for Edge. On iPhone and iPad, every browser uses Apple's. Their privacy policies apply. I-5 Buddy gets back only the words, uses them on your phone to pick a command or an answer, and never saves or sends them. The microphone is on only while the listening panel is open, for 20 seconds at most, and it stops when you switch away from the app.

Spoken narration

Whenever the app talks

I-5 Buddy talks in its own natural voice, in English and in Spanish (Settings > Language). We recorded every line ahead of time with a speech service on our Cloudflare account (Deepgram's Aura voices), using only the app's own text, so nothing you do is sent anywhere to make the voice. The stories for kids (Settings > Stories for kids) were recorded the same way. The app plays those recordings. The website downloads each recording from i5.dominares.org as it's needed (or all at once from Settings > Offline) and keeps it. The iPhone app has the recordings built in, except the long stories from Tell me more, which it downloads from i5.dominares.org the first time, and it downloads any other recording it doesn't carry the same way. The recordings your phone asks for show which landmark or story is playing, and in which language, so Cloudflare could tell roughly where you are on I-5. Our server keeps no log of them.

When a recording is missing and can't be downloaded, or when you pick another voice in Settings > Narration > Voice, I-5 Buddy speaks with a voice your browser provides (in Spanish, a Spanish voice). On iPhone, the voices run on the phone itself. On Android, it depends on your phone's speech engine. On a computer, some voices are online: Chrome's voices named "Google …" and Edge's named "… Online (Natural)". With one of those, your browser sends the words to Google or Microsoft. The words can say where you are, like "near mile 309, heading north". Without the natural voice, I-5 Buddy uses a voice that runs on your device. It uses an online voice only if you pick one, or if your browser has no other. Settings > Narration > Voice marks those voices (online).

Sharing

Only when you tap Share

Share, Copy, Text message and Email hand the text to your phone's share sheet, messages or email app, or clipboard. I-5 Buddy sends nothing itself. A shared link holds the app's address, or one landmark's (i5.dominares.org/#place=…), never your location. The trip picture in Passport is drawn on your phone and goes only where you send it. It shows your trip's date, miles, regions and destination, your stamp and badge counts, and your ride and its nickname. A strip of I-5 on it marks where the trip started and ended, or where you are now if the trip is still going. The all-time picture marks every mile of I-5 you've driven.

Links to other sites

Only when you tap one

Links such as weather.gov forecasts, Caltrans QuickMap, Apple Maps, Google Maps, the missions' own websites, Wikipedia, Wikimedia Commons, the App Store and dominares.org open those sites. On an iPhone or iPad, i5.dominares.org/get opens the App Store by itself. Those sites see your visit like any other, and can tell you came from I-5 Buddy. A weather.gov link includes the point ahead that its forecast or alert is for, and an Apple Maps link includes the landmark's location. A mission's Directions link opens Apple Maps on an iPhone, iPad or Mac and Google Maps elsewhere, with the mission's location as the destination. Your maps app then works out the way from where you are, under its own policy.

Checking for updates in the iPhone app

In the iPhone app only, at most once a day

The iPhone app asks Apple's public App Store lookup (itunes.apple.com) which version of I-5 Buddy is in the App Store, so it can tell you when an update is ready. The request asks only about the app's own App Store listing. It sends nothing about you: no location, no trips, no stamps and no settings. As with any web request, Apple sees your IP address and that the request came from the app. The answer is not sent anywhere else. In a browser, I-5 Buddy never makes this request: the website updates itself.

Error reports in Chrome and Edge

Only when something fails

Cloudflare asks Chrome, Edge and other browsers built on Chrome to report requests to i5.dominares.org that fail. A request fails, for example, when the connection drops or the server answers with an error. Such a report goes from your browser to Cloudflare. It holds the address that failed, the page it came from, the kind of error and its code, timings, your IP address and your browser type. Successful visits are not reported.

What stays on your phone

I-5 Buddy saves these in your browser's storage for i5.dominares.org, or for sugplu.github.io in a copy added from the old address. The iPhone app keeps the same things inside the app on your iPhone. The Android app keeps them in your browser's storage for i5.dominares.org, like the website. They are kept on your phone, not on our server, and we can't see them:

  • Your settings: language, voice, speed, theme, narration choices, and Live road info on or off.
  • Your last spot on I-5 (the mile and direction, not GPS coordinates), so the app can pick up where you left off.
  • Your passport: a stamp with the time you first passed each landmark, your badges, and which miles of I-5 you've driven.
  • Your mission passport: for each of the 21 California missions you've stamped, the time and whether your phone was there or you marked it visited yourself (no GPS coordinates), and when the app last welcomed you at one.
  • Whether you've seen the "New in I-5 Buddy" card, and in the iPhone app when it last checked the App Store and the version it found.
  • Your last 30 trips: when, where on I-5 each started and ended, miles, top speed and destination.
  • Your destination, your recent destinations and your Home and Work places, and your navigation choices.
  • Your ride (vehicle, color, badge and nickname), your game scores, your license plate finds and the names you give players in Car Party.
  • The last live road info it got, including points on the road ahead of you from the past week.
  • For feedback: an unsent draft, and after you send, your name and email to fill in the form next time.
  • Offline copies of the app, of the photos you've viewed or saved, and of the voice's recordings.

Your raw GPS readings and what you say to the microphone are never saved. They're gone when you close the app. Your phone or browser works out the GPS position with its own location service. That service may contact its maker, such as Apple or Google, under its own policy. I-5 Buddy sends your position only as described under Directions.

Clearing it

  • Settings > Reset > Clear passport deletes your stamps, trips and badges.
  • To erase everything, clear the website data for i5.dominares.org in your browser's settings. If you used the old address, clear sugplu.github.io too. If you added I-5 Buddy to your Home Screen, remove it there too.
  • In the iPhone app, deleting the app erases everything it saved.
  • In the Android app, delete the site data for i5.dominares.org in your browser's settings (in Chrome: Settings, Site settings, All sites). Uninstalling the app may not erase it.

Who we share information with

  • We don't sell or rent your information, and we don't share it for advertising.
  • Our service providers are Cloudflare (our server, the feedback database and the error reports), Geoapify (place search and routes, asked by our server) and GitHub. GitHub Pages stores the app's files and serves the old sugplu.github.io address. They handle the requests described above. Cloudflare runs our server and the feedback database for us under its data processing terms: it may use that information only to provide its service to us, and it must protect it at least as well as this notice describes.
  • Mail you send to support@dominares.org passes through Cloudflare Email Routing to the mailbox where we read it. Our email provider keeps those emails and our replies, including replies to your feedback.
  • The National Weather Service, Caltrans, OpenFreeMap, Amazon Web Services and your browser's speech services (Google, Apple or Microsoft) get requests straight from your phone when you use those features. In Safari on an iPhone or iPad, Apple's App Store also gets Safari's request for its banner for the iPhone app, and the iPhone app asks Apple's App Store lookup for the app's current version once a day at most. Their own privacy policies apply.
  • We may disclose information if the law requires it, for example to answer a valid court order.

How long we keep it, and your choices

  • Feedback messages stay until we delete them. Nothing deletes them automatically. After we delete one, Cloudflare's automatic database history can still restore it for up to 30 days.
  • Spam-check codes and the daily random values are kept at least two days, and deleted when we next accept a message after that (see Send feedback). The database history can restore them for up to 30 days after that.
  • On your phone, everything stays until you clear it, the app trims it (30 trips, a week of weather lookups), or your browser clears it.
  • Other services keep what they receive under their own policies.

To see, correct or delete a message you sent, email support@dominares.org from the address you used. If you write from a different address, tell us which one you used. We'll email that address to check it's you before we share or delete anything. We'll tell you what we have and delete it if you ask, and we answer within 45 days. We don't edit stored messages, so to correct one, we delete it and you can send a new one.

Do Not Track

I-5 Buddy doesn't track you across websites. So it works the same whether or not your browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal. It has no ads, analytics or tracking code, and we don't let any other company add any. The services listed above get your IP address when your phone contacts them, and they keep it under their own policies. We can't control whether they link it to your visits to other sites that use them.

Children

I-5 Buddy is not meant for children under 13 to use on their own, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you think a child under 13 has sent us a feedback message, email support@dominares.org and we'll delete it.
