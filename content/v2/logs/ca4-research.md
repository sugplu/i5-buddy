# ca4: researcher's notes

48 places, 9 zones, 12 trivia.

ca4 (CA exits 215-330) is written: 48 places, 9 zones with no gaps (215, 221, 234, 246, 262, 278, 292, 309, 319, 330) and 12 trivia. The JSON is valid and lint_content.py reports 0 errors and 0 warnings across all content files. Using the app's positioning rule (coordinates within 4 miles, otherwise the exit), the biggest gap between places is about 7.8 miles (280 to 288).

Changes to the existing places:
- Dropped 'Why it's called the Grapevine' and 'Tejon Ranch' because ca3 already covers both (its Grapevine grade place and its Tejon Ranch cattle place). The Grapevine name survives as a trivia question at exit 215, now sourced to Wikipedia's Tejon Pass article.
- Dropped 'Wasco, rose town'. I found no readable source for the 55 percent figure or the Rose Capital claim: the Wasco Wikipedia article and the city site say nothing about roses.
- Removed details I could not support: the claim that drivers mistake the Wind Gap plant for Edmonston, ranch workers leaving notes in the Buttonwillow tree, the artificial kit fox dens near Lost Hills (the NFWF page has no such detail), the Tesla charger lounge, and the 444-mile aqueduct length.
- Corrected two details: the Kern Water Bank is about 20,600 acres (32 square miles), not 20,000, and the Lakeview Gusher's peak flow is left out because the sources disagree (90,000 vs 100,000 barrels a day).
- Every other existing place was kept, rechecked and expanded.

Things I was unsure about:
1. The 1995 I-5 bridge collapse (7 killed) has only one source: Wikipedia's List of bridge failures, under Arroyo Pasajero near Coalinga. I placed it at the Los Gatos Creek crossing (mile 327.4, from OpenStreetMap). There is a second pair of bridges about 0.7 mile north, so the exact span is not certain.
2. That same place mentions the 1948 Deportee plane crash and El Camino Viejo's Poso de Chane stop. The crash happened in the Los Gatos Canyon hills about 20 miles west of Coalinga, so the ca5 researcher may cover it too.
3. The exits for some far-off places are my best guess at the route: Allensworth (exit 305, about 30 miles away, past Alpaugh) and Kern National Wildlife Refuge (exit 288).
4. I could not confirm that Blackwells Corner is still a working stop, so it is filed under history rather than as a stop.
5. Several visible/spot settings are judgment calls: Elk Hills about 7.8 miles away, oil pumps on the Lost Hills about 3.5 miles away, orchards at exit 239, the Midway and Gates substations, and the county line sign.
6. I found no source with crop months for pistachio, almond, garlic or tomato harvests (the UC, Almond Board and pistachio grower pages were blocked or silent). The only harvest season used is 'pistachios are shaken each fall', from NBC Bay Area. The tule fog months (November through March) and tule elk months (August through October) are sourced.
7. The 1985 Kettleman Hills earthquake is listed as magnitude 5.9 in one source and 6.1 in another, so the text says 'about magnitude 6'.
8. The Kettleman Hills landfill and environmental justice place is worded strictly from Wikipedia. The state investigation's findings are not stated because I did not read that report.
9. Crop figures come from the USDA's county agricultural commissioner data (2020 to 2022 CSV files), because the Kern County agriculture sites were blocked.
10. The 1991 dust storm happened north of Coalinga, so it was left to ca5.
