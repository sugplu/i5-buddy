# ca3 fact-check log (CA exits 140-215)

Checked 2026-11-10 by the adversarial fact-checker. I read every src and src2 myself with wiki.py full or WebFetch. I also ran where.py on every coordinate.

**Totals:** 40 places checked. 37 places changed, 3 left as they were (Lockheed, the valley wheat entry, The Death Valley rescue). 0 places deleted, because each one's core claim held up. 12 trivia checked, 1 fixed. 9 zones checked, 6 had text or season notes changed. Zones run 140 to 215 with no gaps or overlaps (145, 153, 162, 168, 176, 191, 203, 210).

Extra sources used to settle claims (all read this session): Wikipedia Taylor Yard, Los Angeles flood of 1938, Griffith Park Zoo, California condor, Transcontinental Air Transport, C. Alfred "Chief" Anderson, Amelia Earhart, José María Verdugo, Glendale (California), Convento Building (Mission San Fernando), 1971 San Fernando earthquake, Pioneer Oil Refinery, Valencia (Santa Clarita), Templin Highway, U.S. Route 99 in California, Post Fire, January 2025 Southern California wildfires (Hughes Fire), Mount Pinos, The Umbrellas (Christo and Jeanne-Claude), Tejon Indian Tribe, Hesperoyucca whipplei. Official pages: OHP landmark 653 (The Cascades), parks.ca.gov Fort Tejon, hartpark.santaclarita.gov, tataviam.land, tejonranch.com, ridgeroute.org, damfailures.org, the Hungry Valley wildflower booklet PDF (text decoded locally) and the Caltrans Operation Snowflake flyer PDF (text decoded locally). ## Places

1. **Los Angeles River.** In "more", "the stretch is Paayme Paxaayt" became "called the river Paayme Paxaayt". Wikipedia LA River gives the Tongva name for the whole river, not the Narrows. The groundwater reason for the unpaved bottom is confirmed by Wikipedia Taylor Yard ("unlined channel bottom due to the high groundwater table ... sand bars"). The 1938 flood was deadly (113 to 115 killed, LA flood of 1938). Kept.
2. **Griffith Park.** Spot: "brown, brushy hills" became "steep, brushy hills", because the hills are green in winter and spring. The claim that the zoo was built on the ostrich farm site is confirmed by Wikipedia Griffith Park Zoo. The acreage and "largest urban-wilderness municipal park" match the Griffith Observatory page (city). No other change.
3. **Zoo, Autry and Travel Town.** In "more", the aerodrome sentence now says pioneers "used" the field from 1912 to 1939, not that each of them flew there until 1939 (Wikipedia Griffith Park). "More than 5,000 people lived there until it closed" became "at its peak more than 5,000". Rodger Young Village gives 5,000 only as the peak. The condor line now reads "after the last wild condors were captured in 1987 ... one of two places where the survivors were bred". Wikipedia California condor gives both 27 and 22 birds, so I dropped the number.
4. **Grand Central Air Terminal.** Removed "Amelia Earhart bought her first plane here". Wikipedia Amelia Earhart says she bought her first plane, a Kinner Airster, in 1921 at Kinner Field. Grand Central's predecessor did not open until 1923. Replaced "Lindbergh piloted the nation's first regularly scheduled coast-to-coast flight" with the July 1929 Lindbergh Line: a 48-hour trip with planes by day and trains by night, ending at Grand Central. The service was air and rail, not an all-air flight (Wikipedia Transcontinental Air Transport, now src2). The Forsythe and Anderson flight is now a "round-trip flight across the country by Black pilots" (Wikipedia C. Alfred "Chief" Anderson). "grand railroad station" became "classic railroad station" (source wording).
5. **Verdugo Mountains.** "Verdugo grazed cattle" is now sourced and made concrete: "he soon had nearly 2,000 head of cattle" (Wikipedia Glendale; José María Verdugo). Burbank's 1867 purchase now comes before the 1871 Great Partition. "Villages stood in the valleys around these mountains" became "villages stood beside springs in its canyons" (Wikipedia Verdugo Mountains: "villages at some springs in the canyons").
6. **Lockheed's hidden factory.** All claims confirmed (Wikipedia Lockheed Corporation, Burbank, Hollywood Burbank Airport). No change.
7. **Sun Valley's thorny name.** Fixed the grammar so the name belongs to the village: "a Fernandeño Tongva village whose name means...". "Runs only during storms" became "carries much water only during and after storms" (Wikipedia Tujunga Wash).
8. **When the valley grew wheat.** Confirmed. The 1876 "largest wheat area" line comes from Wikipedia Burbank. Pacoima crops come from Wikipedia Pacoima. No change.
9. **Pacoima, Ritchie Valens' hometown.** "a Tataviam word" became "the name of a Native village here". Wikipedia Pacoima calls the word Tataviam in its lead but Fernandeño in its history, so I did not pick one. In "more", replaced the Valens park line with a sourced detail that fits this exact stretch: I-5 between the 170 and 118 freeways is the Ritchie Valens Memorial Highway (Wikipedia Ritchie Valens). Other Valens facts confirmed.
10. **Mission San Fernando.** "a long row of arches" became "a row of 20 arches" (Wikipedia Convento Building). In "more", removed "about 70 acres of olive trees" because it repeated the Sylmar entry. Replaced it with "made about 2,000 gallons of wine" at its peak. The source says that peak applies to wine output, not to the vines, so "at their peak" no longer describes the grapevines. The Native workers sentence now reads "Native people of the mission ... tended" (the Achooykomenga wording described the earlier rancho). Child death figures confirmed (Wikipedia San Fernando, California).
11. **The dam that almost failed.** "evacuated for three days" became "for days". Wikipedia Van Norman Dams says three days and 1971 San Fernando earthquake says four. "Built of hydraulic fill" became "built largely of hydraulic fill" (the dam also had rolled fill). "Engineers later estimated" became "It was later estimated", since the source does not say who made the estimate.
12. **Sylmar's sea of olives.** "By 1894" became "In 1894" (source). "Had become the largest olive grove in the world" is now worded as a claim: "were said to be". "More" confirmed.
13. **Power from the Columbia River.** In "more", removed the repeated "3.1 gigawatts". It now reads "That is enough power for two to three million households" (Wikipedia Pacific DC Intertie).
14. **Los Angeles Aqueduct Cascades.** Removed "about six years" and "up to 3,900 workers at a time". Sources disagree: construction began in 1905, 1907 or 1908, and the payroll peaked at 6,060. It now says "thousands of workers and 164 tunnels". "Most San Fernando Valley farm towns" became "several" (Wikipedia San Fernando Valley). The 1940 extension sentence is now tied to OHP landmark 653 (Tioga Pass intake, 338 miles), which is the new src2.
15. **Newhall Pass Interchange.** "1930 bypass ... that replaced the old road over the pass" became "built by 1930 to avoid Newhall Pass itself". The old pass road survives as SR 14 (Wikipedia I-5 in California).
16. **Oak of the Golden Dream.** "First authenticated gold discovery" is now worded as the state plaque's claim. Wikipedia Rancho San Francisco notes earlier finds about 30 years before. "Clinging to the roots" became "clinging to the onion roots" so it cannot be heard as oak roots. "Came to work the canyon" became "came to mine the area", because the 2,000 miners came to the rancho as a whole.
17. **Beale's Cut.** Removed "his horse". The sources say Mix was "filmed jumping over the pass" and do not mention the horse. "Historians still argue" became "film historians have long debated" (source wording). Fixed the unclear "it" so it is the cut, not the tunnel, that partly collapsed in 1994.
18. **William S. Hart Park.** The quote is no longer framed as said in the bequest. It now reads "Before he died he said" (Wikipedia William S. Hart). In "more", removed "Newhall itself was built by the Southern Pacific..." because it repeated The Death Valley rescue entry. Added "He came to the movies from the Shakespearean stage in 1914, when he was nearly 50" (Wikipedia Hart; hartpark.santaclarita.gov says "around age 50"). "Paintings by Charles Russell" became "works by the artist Charles Russell" (source wording). The city's guided house tours are confirmed by hartpark.santaclarita.gov, and the museum reopened April 10, 2026.
19. **Pico Number 4 oil well.** "The longest continually operating oil well in the world" is now worded as a claim: "is often called". "Mentry banned drinking" became "reportedly banned" (Wikipedia Mentryville). "Oldest existing refinery" is confirmed by Wikipedia Pioneer Oil Refinery.
20. **Tataviam homeland.** "Is the heart" became "is considered the heart" (source: "believed to be the center"). "Moving between summer and winter settlements" became "with summer and winter settlements". Removed "Today the tribe is working toward federal recognition", which no source states. It now says "The tribe is not federally recognized" (Wikipedia Tataviam). The 1769 village and wedding are confirmed by Wikipedia Valencia, Santa Clarita. About 20 villages are confirmed by Wikipedia Santa Clarita. 550+ acres and the 2018 founding are confirmed by tataviam.land.
21. **Santa Clara River.** Moved the coordinates from 34.42617,-118.56924 (a point 0.8 mile east of I-5) to the actual freeway crossing, 34.4258,-118.5847. That point comes from OpenStreetMap river geometry, and where.py puts it at exit-mile 170.2. This also confirms "near Magic Mountain Parkway". Added "Here its bed is dry most of the year" (Wikipedia Santa Clara River). "More" confirmed.
22. **Six Flags Magic Mountain.** Removed the exact "still runs 16" coaster count. Wikipedia lists closures in 2025 and 2026 and the permanent X2 closure in September 2026, so the count is changing. It now says "still has one of the biggest collections of coasters anywhere". "Looney Tunes characters first appeared in its very first season" became "it had permission to use Looney Tunes characters from its very first season" (source wording).
23. **The Death Valley rescue.** All confirmed (CHL 556 text in Wikipedia Rancho San Francisco; 2 dollars an acre in Wikipedia Santa Clarita). No change.
24. **St. Francis Dam disaster.** "Second-deadliest" is now worded as a claim: "is considered" (damfailures.org says it, behind Johnstown). The exit stays at 172 even though where.py gives exit-mile 177.1. The dam site is 7.2 miles off the freeway, the "more" text is about the flood passing the I-5/126 junction, and Newhall Ranch Road at exit 172 leads toward San Francisquito Canyon.
25. **Val Verde, the Black Palm Springs.** The pool "still opens" was the original Olympic pool. Wikipedia says the 1994 earthquake damaged it and it was "repaired and shortened", so the text now says so.
26. **Castaic Lake and its name.** "Kids may know the lake from TV" became "Fans of the Mighty Morphin Power Rangers may know the lake", since the show is from the 1990s. Added the snow-closure turnaround gate in the median just north of Lake Hughes Road (Wikipedia I-5 in California). Hughes Fire figures (10,425 acres, 30,000+ under evacuation orders) are confirmed by Wikipedia January 2025 Southern California wildfires.
27. **Five Mile Grade crossover.** Coordinates were mid-grade at 34.53293,-118.65129, which where.py puts at exit-mile 179.7, 3.7 from exit 176. I moved them to the southern crossover bridge ("S5-N5 Sep", OpenStreetMap) at 34.5023,-118.6274, exit-mile 177.2. "The four-lane expressway finished between 1948 and 1952" became "the old four-lane Ridge Route Alternate, widened between 1948 and 1952" (Wikipedia I-5 in California; Ridge Route).
28. **The old Ridge Route.** The 697 turns and 97 circles are scoped to "between Castaic and Gorman", as ridgeroute.org states them. Wikipedia gives 109.5 circles for the whole route. "In places the speed limit was 15" became "Between Castaic and Quail Lake" (Wikipedia Ridge Route). Removed "concrete 4 and a half inches thick". Sources conflict (ridgeroute.org says 4.5 inches, Wikipedia says 4), so it now says "paved with concrete by 1921". The Templin Highway naming is confirmed by Wikipedia Templin Highway (renamed in 1967 from Warm Springs Road).
29. **Highway 99 under the lake.** Removed "the freeway runs almost 1,000 feet above the old canyon", which no source supports. "Opened up the canyon" became "opened through the canyon". "Dust Bowl families traveled it in search of farm work" became "Dust Bowl migrant farm workers traveled it across the state" (Wikipedia U.S. Route 99 in California, which also confirms "Main Street of California").
30. **Vista del Lago.** "Sits high above" became "sits above" (source wording). Removed the Post Fire sentence, which repeated the Gorman entry. Added the Castaic Power Plant comparison: six 250-megawatt main units against Hoover Dam's largest at 130 MW (Wikipedia Castaic Power Plant). Not resolved: DWR's own pages block automated access, so I could not confirm the visitor center is open today. Wikipedia describes it in the present tense. A person should check before launch.
31. **Quail Lake on the fault.** Confirmed ("slices directly through Gorman", Wikipedia Gorman). Added one sourced detail: one of only two deaths reported in 1857 was a woman in Gorman, killed by a collapsing adobe house (Wikipedia 1857 Fort Tejon earthquake).
32. **Gorman and its wildflowers.** The "text" stays: "a trading crossroads" is confirmed by Wikipedia Tejon Pass. In "more", James Gorman's Fort Tejon job is now "reportedly" (source). Added the 1991 Christo and Jeanne-Claude Umbrellas: 1,760 yellow umbrellas around Gorman and Tejon Pass, and the show closed early after a woman was crushed by a windblown umbrella (Wikipedia Gorman; The Umbrellas). The Hungry Valley tours (mid-April through May, by appointment), the 19,000+ acres and "poppies close on cloudy, windy days and at night" are confirmed by decoding the booklet PDF. Months [4, 5] are kept, because the booklet sources them.
33. **Tejon Pass summit.** Removed "Chains are not used, because the snow is often limited to the summit and there is no room ... to take them off". I decoded the Caltrans flyer PDF and it does not say this, and neither does the Caltrans winter tips page. Replaced it with the sourced reason: the Highway Patrol usually closes the freeway outright rather than require chains, because of the steep grade and the heavy car and truck traffic (Wikipedia I-5 in California). Removed "usually for four to six hours" here. That fact moved to the zone note, so it is not said twice.
34. **Frazier Mountain.** "Lumber ... went into building Fort Tejon" became "was cut ... for the new Army post at Fort Tejon" (source: lumber "for use at" the fort). The bird-of-prey line is now "is considered home to one of California's most significant populations" (Wikipedia Mount Pinos). Visible stays true: it is an 8,017-foot mountain 5.4 miles away.
35. **Castac Lake, the eye.** "A creek's fan of gravel" became "the fan of sediment washed down by Cuddy Creek" (source: alluvial fan of Cuddy Creek). "30 miles south" became "about 30 miles south".
36. **Tejon Ranch cattle.** "Set aside about 240,000 acres" became "was designed to protect" (178,000 acres set aside plus an option on 62,000). "The largest such pact" is now worded as "was called". "Were left open" became "the company agreed to leave". "Also farms almonds, pistachios and wine grapes" became "A little less than 6,000 acres grow pistachios, almonds, wine grapes, alfalfa and now olives" (tejonranch.com).
37. **Fort Tejon.** Removed "the first military fort in California's interior". Neither Wikipedia nor parks.ca.gov says it, so it now reads "on the main route between the Central Valley and Southern California" (parks.ca.gov). The 1857 quake sentence no longer repeats the date and magnitude given in the Quail Lake entry. "Civil War reenactments" became "living history demonstrations" (parks.ca.gov wording). The 300-year-old oaks are confirmed by parks.ca.gov.
38. **Peter Lebeck's oak.** "In the late 1800s" became "In 1890" (Wikipedia Peter Lebeck). "French five-franc coin" became "five-franc coin" (source).
39. **The Grapevine grade.** Coordinates were at exit-mile 212.5, 2.5 from exit 210. I moved them to an I-5 point on the grade at 34.894,-118.9172, exit-mile 211.8. Removed "Gravel" from the escape ramps (source says only "escape ramps"). Removed "Caltrans warns that road surfaces can be 4 to 10 degrees colder than the air", which is in neither the flyer nor the Caltrans tips page. Removed "The first downhill gravel arrester bed in the United States was tested ... near Siskiyou Summit in Oregon", which no source I read supports and which is off this stretch. Added the name origin: in 1772 Pedro Fages named it Cañada de las Uvas for its wild grapevines (Wikipedia Tejon Pass). Added that Dead Man's Curve can still be seen from Digier Road (Wikipedia Ridge Route). Spot reworded to match.
40. **California's first reservation.** The text now names the living tribe in its own terms: the Tejon Indian Tribe, a federally recognized tribe of Kitanemuk, Yokuts, Paiute and Chumash people based in Bakersfield (Wikipedia Tejon Indian Tribe, now src2). Replaced "About 2,500 people were reported living there in 1854". Beale's figure was found to be inflated: his successor counted about 800. It now uses the state marker's range of 500 to 2,000. "Farmed wheat, barley and corn with water from Tejon Creek" became "planted wheat, barley and corn, and Tejon Creek watered gardens, vineyards and orchards" (source). Added José Pacheco's April 1864 letter, paraphrased, about agents keeping and selling provisions while his people went hungry. "At gunpoint" and "about a hundred stayed" are confirmed by Wikipedia Tejon Indian Tribe. "One Kitanemuk family still lives on the ranch" is confirmed by Wikipedia Kitanemuk. Removed the acorn line from this entry; it now lives in the 210-215 zone note.
