Buttonwillow on I-5 in California

Buttonwillow sits along Interstate 5 around exits 257 to 263, with the town named for a single tree that turns out to be a buttonbush, not a willow. The freeway crosses the Kern River near the Kern Water Bank's recharge basins and runs past the low, bare Elk Hills. Gas, EV charging, food and lodging are mapped in the data at exit 257, as listed in OpenStreetMap and not live.

Questions drivers ask

What exit is Buttonwillow on I-5?

Exit 263 is signed for Buttonwillow and McKittrick, and exit 257 (CA 58) is also signed for Buttonwillow, McKittrick and Bakersfield. Exit 262 is 7th Standard Road and Rowlee Road.

Is there gas, EV charging, food or a hotel at Buttonwillow on I-5?

At exit 257 the data lists 9 gas stations (brands include Sinclair, Arco, Chevron and EZ Trip), ChargePoint, Rivian and Tesla Supercharger networks, 13 food places and 4 lodging places such as Econo Lodge and Motel 6. This comes from OpenStreetMap as mapped, not live, so it does not show what is open now.

Is there a rest area near Buttonwillow on I-5?

The Buttonwillow Rest Area is at mile 259.2, about 1.4 miles south of town, and serves both directions. Going north, the next listed rest area is Coalinga-Avenal at mile 320.2, about 59.6 miles on.

Why is Buttonwillow called Buttonwillow?

The town is named for a single tree, and it is not a willow at all but a buttonbush. The tree is listed near exit 262.

What is there to see near Buttonwillow on I-5?

Near exit 263, on the left going north, are the McKittrick Tar Pits, where natural asphalt oozes from the oil field below. Buttonwillow was also a headquarters of Miller and Lux, the company of Henry Miller, known as the Cattle King of California.
