Anderson on I-5 in California

Anderson is a railroad town: in 1872 rancher and hotel keeper Elias Anderson deeded the Central Pacific Railroad a right of way, and the railroad named its depot after him. Central Anderson and the Lassen National Park turnoff are at exit 668, with more services at the neighboring exits. Gas, EV, food and lodging here are as mapped in OpenStreetMap, not live.

Questions drivers ask

What exit is Anderson on I-5?

Central Anderson and Lassen National Park are at exit 668. Exits 667, 667A and 667B serve Deschutes Road and Factory Outlet Drive, and exit 670 is Riverside Avenue.

Where can I get gas and EV charging in Anderson on I-5?

Exit 668 has 7 mapped gas stations, including Chevron, Marathon, Maverik and Safeway, plus ChargePoint and Electrify America charging. Exit 667B lists an Arco and a Tesla Supercharger, and exit 667 lists a Shell. All of this is as mapped, not live.

Where can I eat or stay in Anderson off I-5?

Exit 668 lists 14 mapped food places, including Bartels Giant Burger, Burger King and McDonald's, and 3 lodging places such as Best Western and Motel 6. Exit 667B lists 7 food places, and exit 667 lists a Baymont. Listings come from OpenStreetMap and are not live.

Can I see Lassen Peak from I-5 at Anderson?

On clear days, the big mountain on the eastern skyline near exit 668 is Lassen Peak, about 40 miles away. It is the southernmost active volcano in the Cascade Range, and it sits on the right going north.

Where is the nearest rest area to Anderson on I-5?

South of town, Herbert S. Miles Rest Area is at mile 654.1, 13.9 miles back, and serves both directions. North of town, O'Brien Rest Area is at mile 692.9, 24.9 miles ahead.
