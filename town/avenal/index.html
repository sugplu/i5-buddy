Avenal on I-5 in California

Avenal is reached from I-5 at exit 319, where Highway 269 crosses the Kettleman Hills. The name is Spanish for oat field, given by Spanish soldiers and explorers for the wild oats that once covered the plain. The freeway here runs through the West Side farmland of Fresno County.

What to see near Avenal

    Avenal, the oat field

    Highway 269 crosses the Kettleman Hills to Avenal, whose name is Spanish for oat field. Spanish soldiers and explorers named it for the wild oats, waist high, that covered the plain like golden silk. Full story

  2. Exit 319 · on your left going north · History

    The 1928 Kettleman gusher

    In October 1928, after 19 months of drilling, the Milham Exploration Company's Elliott Number 1 well struck oil more than 7,000 feet under the Kettleman Hills. Full story

  3. Exit 322 · Orchards & farms

    Garlic country

    Fresno County grows most of the garlic in the United States, and west side towns such as Huron picked up the slack as Gilroy, once the country's biggest garlic producer, ran out of room. Full story

Fresno County's West Side

The freeway runs through the West Side farmland of Fresno County, which in 2022 led California counties in the value of its almonds, pistachios, garlic and processing tomatoes.

Questions drivers ask

What exit is Avenal on I-5?

Avenal is at exit 319, signed CA 269; Lassen Avenue.

Is there gas and food at the Avenal exit?

The data lists two gas stations at exit 319, with brands shown as 76 and Gas. It also lists three food places there, including Cinnabon, El Sabroso and Subway. These come from OpenStreetMap as mapped, not live, so hours and availability are not shown.

Where is the nearest rest area to Avenal on I-5?

The Coalinga-Avenal Rest Area is at mile 320.2, about 1.2 miles north of Avenal, serving both directions. Heading south, the Buttonwillow Rest Area is at mile 259.2, about 59.8 miles away, also serving both directions.

What does the name Avenal mean?

Avenal is Spanish for oat field. Spanish soldiers and explorers named it for the waist-high wild oats that covered the plain like golden silk.

What is there to see near Avenal on I-5?

The 1928 Kettleman gusher is at mile 317.3 on the left going north, where the Milham Exploration Company's Elliott Number 1 well struck oil more than 7,000 feet under the Kettleman Hills. Farther north, exit 322 is garlic country: Fresno County grows most of the garlic in the United States.
